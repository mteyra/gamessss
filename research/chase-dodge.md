# Chase and dodge: editorial evidence log

Researched 14 November 2026. This collection contains **12 chase games and 12 dodgeball relatives**, written as original, playable starter guides. Names identify game families rather than a claim that every school uses identical rules. Player counts, ages, durations, round limits, tie procedures and care suggestions are hosting recommendations unless explicitly described as documented. “Worldwide” describes contemporary play, not a verified origin.

The curation favours different mechanics: switching the chased person, growing teams, shrinking search parties, static obstacles, changing routes, cooperative rescues, protected targets, target-based revivals, outside-court participation and eliminator-linked returns. Ordinary tag is not repeated under several local names just to raise the count.

## User memories resolved

- **Islands:** PE Universe explicitly describes pairs lying together and the outside person leaving when a runner lies beside the pair. Urban Initiatives documents the same lying variant under Train Tag. The atlas retains that mechanic and adds a brief grace period and cat rotation. The standing option is supported by the linked partner-tag version.
- **Manhunt / one-touch-touch-all:** the displayed game uses infection or build-up rules. The Manhunt IRL guide documents caught runners joining the hunters. The phrase “one-touch-touch-all” remains marked as the user's remembered name, with no claim of a standard international alias.
- **Basketball rescue:** Elementary PE Games documents a basket freeing the entire waiting team. The atlas omits upright mats and elevated benches, uses floor rescue zones, and explicitly simplifies catches.
- **Noodle goalkeeper:** no sufficiently clear authoritative source was found for the precise remembered variant. The atlas therefore labels its playable rules **house rules, inspired by the user**. The S&S link establishes use of a pool noodle in a dodgeball variant; it does not establish this goalkeeper scoring system.

## Deliberate boundaries

These rules are not tournament regulations. Most games are transmitted orally and have legitimate competing versions. The source links let readers compare those versions. The dataset uses `starter` when clarifying or adapting a sourced game; `house` is used for the newly composed Noodle Keeper rules. No game is rated “world's best,” and no inventor or country of origin is invented.

Every dodgeball entry specifies a target zone and live/dead-ball behaviour where relevant. Standard throwing entries use soft balls and below-waist targets. Care notes describe specific adjustments, not a claim that any activity is universally appropriate. Shared play should be adjusted with the actual participants, particularly when getting down to the floor, rapid turning, crowding, contact or elimination would exclude someone.

No images, source prose, videos or paywalled manuals were copied into the atlas. The authored text explains mechanics with new wording. Linked sources are third-party resources and may change after this research snapshot.
