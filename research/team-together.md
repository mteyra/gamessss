# GAMESSSS research: team games and playing together

Research checked 14 November 2026. This file accompanies `dist/data/team-together.json`: 12 team/net/territory games and 12 cooperative, reaction, circle and social games.

## Editorial approach

This collection prioritizes a change in the way people interact: shared goals in Tchoukball, three simultaneous teams in KIN-BALL, a change between foot and hand play in Speedball, a moving receiver in Endball, and supporter groups that grow in Roshambo Rockstar. The cooperative section also includes lower-exertion games so a host can vary pace, space and access needs. These are not ranked as objectively "the best" games, and this is not a complete worldwide catalogue.

The displayed player counts, ages, space categories and durations are editorial hosting suggestions. They are not quoted federation specifications. Region means an association or context, not an exclusive ownership or first-invention claim. Text is newly written, concise interpretation; images and article prose were not copied.

A `documented` entry closely follows a described recreational game, with practical facilitation notes. `starter` indicates a short playable introduction with omitted competition detail or a specified short end condition. `house` prominently marks a designed modification. Even a documented recreational game can have local names and rule differences.

## Rules requiring explicit qualification

- **Tchoukball:** Shared targets, no interference and the rule of three are central. The three-consecutive-shots limit applies across both teams, not separately to each team. This is a beginner summary; frame faults, zone exceptions and detailed restarts belong in the linked federation rules.
- **KIN-BALL:** The Singapore summary specifies a 3 m hit; the India teaching page specifies six feet. The atlas states its 3 m introductory convention and does not call it the current complete international ruleset. The full competition also has restrictions on calls and more detailed period formats.
- **Jugger:** The shown house introduction removes chains, wrestling and pinning. It retains one runner, four spar players, scored placement, and five-beat temporary inactivity. It cannot substitute for equipment checking and an experienced introduction to controlled tagging.
- **Roundnet:** This is casual beginner play without current competition boundary, no-drop-zone and advanced service details. Its shorter target is explicitly a house rule. “Spikeball” is a commonly used brand-related search alias, not the generic sport's governing body.
- **Danish Longball:** Sources and schools differ. The chosen PE source uses three outs and a pitcher freeze. Returning unfinished runners to their last safe base is an atlas simplification, stated in the entry. No thrown-ball hits on runners.
- **Newcomb:** Catch-and-throw variants differ over passes, catches, rotations, elimination and scoring. This introduction has direct returns, no elimination and explicit rally restarts.
- **Endball:** The cited UVic version requires everyone to touch the ball, underarm passes and a rotating end-line catcher. The entry supplies stationary possession and turnover handling for a usable introductory round.
- **Speedball:** Sources can mean different sports. Here it is the US school soccer/handball hybrid, not Egyptian speed-ball or speedball paintball. This house version retains the kick-up transition, removes basketball scoring, and uses cone goals/end zones.
- **Korfball:** Official mixed-team and same-gender defending conventions are acknowledged. The playable introduction uses agreed defensive matchups and is labelled a house adaptation. This is not presented as official competition.
- **Sitting Volleyball:** The floor-contact description is simplified. Current ParaVolley rules have specific lifting/contact exceptions; elite competition eligibility differs from a recreational group trying the game. The entry does not imply that sitting play is accessible to every individual.
- **Hooverball:** The historical medicine-ball game and tennis scoring are documented by the US National Park Service. The atlas explicitly offers a light-ball house adaptation with rally scoring; the front-court-to-back-court restriction supplies its distinguishing tactic.
- **Ninja:** Many versions retire one limb or target legs. The entry chooses hand taps only and one-hit elimination in short rounds, with two-hand lives as a labelled optional variation.
- **Human Knot:** Some configurations resolve into multiple loops or cannot be untangled. Recognizing an impossible or uncomfortable configuration is a valid finish, not a failed group.
- **Giants, Wizards & Elves:** The referenced Scouts version awards points without chasing. It is distinct from the chase version and from Roshambo Rockstar's growing supporter groups.
- **Wink Murder:** A two-minute round limit is an added host convention, to avoid long waits for players who have exited.

## Access and evidence notes

The Scouts Human Knot address appeared in search but returned 404 when opened. The public Online Scout Manager entry was readable, describes its version and credits the earlier Scouts activity; it is the cited source. Scouting America's Human Knot and Silent Line-Up pages, the Sense balloon page, and an eCampusOntario theatre page returned 403 to the reader. They are not used as the sole rule evidence. Accessible OSM, Responsive Classroom and The Theater Door pages supplied the relevant descriptions instead.

The national/federation sources offer better authority for their sports than generic listicles. Educational pages still describe particular teaching versions rather than universal rules. The site therefore exposes both the chosen rules and the reason a version is labelled as adapted.

## Hosting notes

Give the physical demonstration before the first live round. Agree boundaries, legal contact, a stop signal and what happens after a fault. Use a short practice round to check that the chosen game fits the actual group. Offer walking, seated, throwing-distance or communication changes collaboratively; an “accessible” label alone cannot guarantee suitability for everyone. Keep the printed entry with the host so disputes can be settled using the version everyone was taught.

No images, logos, federations' full rulebooks or substantial source passages were copied into the atlas. External links are for further reading, not sponsorship or endorsement.
