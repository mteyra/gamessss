# Group Games — the field guide

**72 ways to get together and play.** Research edition: 14 November 2026.

This companion to the Group Games website collects the complete starter rules, game variations and source links in a portable form. It is a curated introduction to physical group games, not an exhaustive inventory or a ranking of the world’s best games.

## How to use the atlas

Choose a game that fits the people, space and equipment you actually have. Explain its aim, demonstrate one turn, agree the boundary and stop signal, and try a short round. Change one rule together when it would improve the experience. The game becomes yours through playing it.

## Research and provenance

The catalogue contains six families of twelve games, drawing on 83 distinct linked source URLs. Sources include governing bodies, cultural and educational organisations, municipal resources, teachers, camp practitioners and reference works. Links support the particular mechanics described; their presence does not imply endorsement by the source.

Text is newly written. Names, mechanics and cultural associations were checked against linked resources where available. No source illustrations or video files have been copied into the site. Every game includes its own rule note so a local or simplified form is not presented as universal.

The current editorial labels are **8 documented games**, **50 starter rulesets** and **14 house adaptations**. “Documented” identifies a described form of play, not a claim of one worldwide rulebook. “Starter” means a concise, usable version with selected clarifications. “House” marks an explicit adaptation or newly composed version; sources may document the parent game rather than the exact adaptation.

Player ranges, session lengths, age suggestions, energy ratings and space categories are hosting guidance. They are not official eligibility rules, measured exertion, or universal accessibility ratings. The upper player count is a suggested practical group size, and may require equal teams or an even number; check the setup. Session duration can include repeated short rounds.

Country and region labels describe associations, not proven places of invention. Traditional games often have multiple names and regional rules. The linked organisations and local players remain the best route to deeper cultural context or formal competition.

The user’s Islands memory is preserved as the lying-pairs chase. “One-touch-touch-all” is retained as a remembered name for infection-style Manhunt without claiming a standard alias. ## Chase & escape

Change who runs, who helps, or which routes remain open. These games show how a single tagging rule can transform a familiar chase.

<a id="islands"></a>

### 1. Islands

*Two is company. Three is a chase.*

Playgrounds & camps worldwide · 8–30 people · 5–10 minutes · Suggested age 8+ · large space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** Island Tag, Cat and Mouse, Partner Switch Tag, Train Tag

**Equipment:** None

The safest place in the room lasts only until somebody joins you. A chase jumps from pair to pair, and people who looked comfortably out of the action suddenly become its main character.

**Set up:** Use an even number: choose one cat and one mouse. Everyone else lies side by side in pairs, with wide running lanes between islands.

1. The cat tries to gently tag the mouse; everybody else stays in their pair.
2. The mouse can escape by slowing down and lying beside either end of a pair.
3. An island holds two: the person at the opposite end gets up and becomes the new mouse.
4. If tagged, the mouse becomes the cat and the old cat becomes the mouse. Give the new mouse three seconds to leave.
5. After a minute, pause and choose a fresh cat so one person does not do all the chasing.

**How it ends:** Play timed rounds; there is no overall winner.

**The twist:** Arriving at safety sends somebody else into danger.

**Mix it up:** Use standing pairs instead: join at one end and release the opposite person.

**Make it work:** No diving or jumping over islands. Use standing pairs when getting down and up is unsuitable.

**Starter rules:** The pair-switch mechanic is documented; the grace period and timed rotation are hosting adaptations.

**Sources:**

- [PE Universe · Island Tag](<https://peuniverse.com/video/island-tag/>)
- [Urban Initiatives · Cat and Mouse Tag](<https://www.uicoach.org/recess-games/catandmouse>)

### 2. Manhunt

*Every person caught makes the hunt bigger.*

Playgrounds & camps worldwide · 6–30 people · 10–20 minutes · Suggested age 8+ · large space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** Build-up Manhunt, Infection, One-touch-touch-all

**Equipment:** None

Hide-and-seek develops a serious trust problem. The friend hiding beside you can become a hunter in seconds, and a quiet start slowly turns into a coordinated search for the last few runners.

**Set up:** Agree clear boundaries, a meeting point and a stop call. Choose one or two hunters and give everyone else one minute to hide.

1. Hunters search within the agreed area; runners may hide or move.
2. A gentle shoulder or back tag turns a runner into a hunter.
3. The tagged player announces the change, counts to five, then joins the search.
4. There are no prisons or rescues in this version. Newly caught players keep playing.
5. Stop when everyone is caught or the timer ends. Regroup, count everyone and rotate the first hunter.

**How it ends:** Hunters win if everyone is caught; runners still free at the timer win together.

**The twist:** The searching team grows with every successful tag.

**Mix it up:** Hosting adaptation: play in daylight with a smaller area and walking-only movement.

**Make it work:** Keep roads, water, buildings and climbable hiding spots outside the boundary. Anyone can call an immediate stop.

**Starter rules:** Uses the infection variant. “One-touch-touch-all” is the user’s remembered name, not a verified universal alias.

**Sources:**

- [Manhunt IRL · Build-up Manhunt](<https://manhuntgame.com/variations/build-up-manhunt>)

### 3. Sardines

*Find the hiding place. Become the hiding place.*

Playgrounds & camps worldwide · 5–20 people · 5–15 minutes · Suggested age 6+ · large space · Energy 1/3

**Also known as:** Reverse Hide-and-Seek

**Equipment:** None

The search party keeps mysteriously disappearing. Finding the hidden player does not end your turn: it gives you a place in an increasingly hard-to-hide crowd trying very hard not to laugh.

**Set up:** Choose one hider and agree accessible hiding areas large enough for the whole group. Everyone else waits at a meeting point.

1. Give the hider one minute to choose a place.
2. The others search independently or in pairs.
3. When you find the hider, quietly join them instead of announcing the discovery.
4. Each finder joins the hiding group until only one searcher remains.
5. End when everyone has found the group, or call everyone back at the agreed time. Rotate the hider.

**How it ends:** The shared reveal ends the round; being last is a funny finale, not a penalty.

**The twist:** The hiding team grows while the searching team shrinks.

**Mix it up:** Pair searchers for younger groups; both join the hiders when they find them.

**Make it work:** Never use locked, cramped or airless hiding spaces. Keep all players within a supervised boundary.

**Starter rules:** Sources differ on who hides next; rotate volunteers in this starter version.

**Sources:**

- [Online Scout Manager · Sardines](<https://www.onlinescoutmanager.co.uk/programme.php?action=view&id=57422>)

### 4. Blob Tag

*One chase. Then two. Then four.*

Playgrounds & camps worldwide · 10–30 people · 5–10 minutes · Suggested age 8+ · large space · Energy 2/3

**Also known as:** Splitting Blob

**Equipment:** None

The challenge is no longer just running away. Chasers have to steer together, and every fourth player triggers a split: suddenly there are two coordinated blobs closing the gaps instead of one.

**Set up:** Two volunteers form the first blob by linking elbows or hands. Everyone else spreads out inside a generous boundary.

1. The blob moves as a unit and tags with its free hands.
2. A tagged player stops safely and joins the blob.
3. At four people, split into two pairs. Both pairs can now chase.
4. Every new blob repeats the same grow-to-four, split-in-two cycle.
5. End when all players belong to blobs. The last two caught can start the next round.

**How it ends:** The round ends when everyone has joined; try a new starting pair each time.

**The twist:** Your opponent multiplies instead of getting longer forever.

**Mix it up:** Hosting adaptation: connected pairs walk while unlinked runners move at a gentle jog.

**Make it work:** Never pull a partner. If a link breaks, stop and reconnect; use loose hand links when elbows are uncomfortable.

**Starter rules:** This uses the splitting-at-four form; the single giant chain is a separate variation.

**Sources:**

- [Playworks · Blob Tag](<https://www.playworks.org/game-library/blob-tag/>)

### 5. Octopus

*Every crossing redraws the ocean.*

Playgrounds & camps worldwide · 8–30 people · 5–10 minutes · Suggested age 6+ · large space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** Octopus Tag, Seaweed Tag

**Equipment:** None

One moving octopus is manageable. A whole sea of stationary tentacles is another story. Each caught runner leaves a new obstacle, so the route that worked last crossing may now be completely closed.

**Set up:** Mark two opposite shores. One octopus starts in the middle; all fish begin behind the same shore.

1. The octopus calls for the fish to cross. All fish travel to the opposite shore.
2. The octopus can move and gently tag fish before they reach safety.
3. Tagged fish stay where caught as stationary crabs, keeping their feet planted.
4. On later crossings, crabs can tag passing fish within easy reach; new catches become crabs too.
5. Repeat crossings until all fish are caught. Reset the ocean and choose a new octopus.

**How it ends:** Celebrate the last fish caught, then begin a fresh round.

**The twist:** The players themselves become the obstacle course.

**Mix it up:** Hosting adaptation: allow crabs to turn on the spot while everyone crosses at walking pace.

**Make it work:** No grabbing, lunging or blocking with feet. Leave room between shores and walls.

**Starter rules:** Uses one roaming octopus and stationary caught players; other local versions differ.

**Sources:**

- [Youth Group Games · Octopus Tag](<https://youthgroupgames.com.au/games/octopus-tag/>)

<a id="shadow-tag"></a>

### 6. Shadow Tag

*Catch the part of me that touches the sun.*

Playgrounds & camps worldwide · 3–14 people · 3–8 minutes · Suggested age 5+ · medium space · Energy 2/3

**Also known as:** Shadow Chase

**Equipment:** None

You are running two races at once: one with your body, one with the shape beside it. The angle of the light changes the tactics, and no player has to touch another.

**Set up:** Use a bright open space with distinct shadows. Choose one tagger and mark a boundary away from traffic.

1. The tagger tries to step on another player’s shadow.
2. Call the player’s name and “caught” when the step lands; everyone plays honestly.
3. That player becomes the tagger. Pause briefly so the previous tagger can move away.
4. Keep exchanging the role until the agreed short timer ends.

**How it ends:** This is continuous play; finish at the timer and swap starting taggers.

**The twist:** Your target moves beside the person you are chasing.

**Mix it up:** Try pairs at walking pace, swapping the chaser after every successful shadow tag.

**Make it work:** Step on shadows, never feet. Choose a new game when shadows become unclear.

**Starter rules:** Uses partner shadow-tag mechanics in a group; pause and time limit are hosting choices.

**Sources:**

- [PE Central · Shadow Tag](<https://www.pecentral.org/lessonideas/ViewLesson.asp?ID=25>)

### 7. Line Tag

*The gym floor becomes a living maze.*

Playgrounds & camps worldwide · 5–16 people · 5–10 minutes · Suggested age 8+ · medium space · Energy 2/3

**Also known as:** Pac Tag, Pac-Man Tag

**Equipment:** None

Painted court markings suddenly become roads, junctions and dead ends. Once tagged players block a route, you have to read the whole map instead of just outrunning the person behind you.

**Set up:** Choose connected court lines and one or two taggers. Walk the allowed routes together before starting.

1. Everyone moves only along the chosen lines, including the taggers.
2. Change direction at intersections; never jump across a gap between lines.
3. If tagged, stop at that point and become a visible roadblock.
4. Runners cannot pass roadblocks; taggers may carefully walk around them and rejoin the line.
5. Reset when one runner remains or after two minutes; choose new taggers and reopen every route.

**How it ends:** Try to be the last runner; everyone rejoins after the short reset.

**The twist:** Getting caught changes the map for everybody else.

**Mix it up:** Hosting adaptation: roadblocks stand beside their point and mark it, rather than sitting on the floor.

**Make it work:** Walk at busy junctions. Never step over another player; use the standing roadblock option when needed.

**Starter rules:** Requires existing connected floor markings; standing roadblocks and timed resets are adaptations.

**Sources:**

- [Playworks · Pac Tag](<https://www.playworks.org/game-library/pac-tag/>)

### 8. Everybody’s It

*You are the hunter. So is literally everyone.*

Playgrounds & camps worldwide · 6–30 people · 3–8 minutes · Suggested age 8+ · medium space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** Everyone’s It Tag

**Equipment:** None

No safe side. No single person to keep an eye on. Everyone is trying to collect tags while slipping away, so the whole game becomes a rapid sequence of tiny, reversible decisions.

**Set up:** Mark a clear space and agree a three-minute round. Everyone starts with a personal score of zero.

1. On “go”, every player may gently tag any other player.
2. Add one to your own count for each successful tag.
3. Being tagged does not freeze or eliminate you; keep moving.
4. Avoid immediate repeated tags on the same person. At the stop signal, freeze and compare scores or personal bests.
5. Reset every score and try another short round.

**How it ends:** Count your tags at the timer; no one sits out.

**The twist:** Attack and defence happen at exactly the same time.

**Mix it up:** Use the documented subtraction variant: lose one point whenever someone tags you.

**Make it work:** Use light shoulder tags and enough room to turn. Walk if the group begins colliding.

**Starter rules:** Uses Playworks’ continuous scoring version; other games with this name use elimination.

**Sources:**

- [Playworks · Everyone’s It Tag](<https://www.playworks.org/game-library/everyones-it-tag/>)

### 9. Stuck in the Mud

*Escaping is easy. Coming back to help is the game.*

Playgrounds & camps worldwide · 8–30 people · 5–10 minutes · Suggested age 6+ · large space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** Freeze Tag, Two-person Rescue Tag

**Equipment:** None

Freeze tag becomes a rescue mission. As the field fills with stranded friends, free runners have to choose between saving themselves and making a coordinated trip back into the most dangerous part of the court.

**Set up:** Choose one or two taggers. Set a two-minute round and let the remaining runners spread out.

1. Taggers gently tag runners below shoulder height.
2. Tagged runners stay still with hands held forward: they are stuck.
3. Two free runners rescue a stuck player together: each touches one shoulder and they clap their free hands together.
4. The rescued runner can move again. Taggers continue until everybody is stuck or time runs out.
5. Rotate taggers and reset the whole group for another round.

**How it ends:** Taggers win by freezing everyone; runners win if anyone remains free at the timer.

**The twist:** Rescue takes two people, so bravery alone is not enough.

**Mix it up:** Hosting adaptation: a pair can rescue by standing beside the player and counting together, without touching.

**Make it work:** No camping beside stuck players. Agree comfortable contact; use the no-touch rescue when preferred.

**Starter rules:** Uses the Scouts/RFU two-rescuer variant, not the familiar crawl-under-legs version.

**Sources:**

- [Scouts · Play stuck in the mud](<https://www.scouts.org.uk/activities/play-stuck-in-the-mud/>)

### 10. Rock–Paper–Scissors Chase

*A hand game with a running commentary.*

Playgrounds & camps worldwide · 4–30 people · 5–10 minutes · Suggested age 6+ · large space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** Rock Paper Scissor Tag, Roshambo Tag

**Equipment:** None

You cannot prepare to sprint until you know whether you won. A tiny hand gesture instantly decides who chases whom, turning a familiar three-choice game into a brilliant test of reaction.

**Set up:** Mark a centre line and a home line on each side. Make two equal lines of players facing a partner at the centre.

1. Each pair plays rock–paper–scissors on the same count. Replay ties.
2. The winner chases; the loser turns toward their own home line.
3. A gentle tag before the home line completes the chase. Reaching home means escape.
4. Both return to their starting side, whether caught or safe.
5. Shift one line along to meet a new opponent and repeat for five rounds.

**How it ends:** Each chase has a catcher or escapee; the starter version has no elimination or cumulative score.

**The twist:** The same person can attack or retreat from one second to the next.

**Mix it up:** Each team can secretly agree one gesture; the losing team runs together and tagged players change teams.

**Make it work:** Use parallel lanes and generous run-off behind home lines. No pushing at the turnaround.

**Starter rules:** Individual-pair rules are the default; team conversion is a documented variation.

**Sources:**

- [Playworks · Rock Paper Scissor Tag](<https://www.playworks.org/game-library/rock-paper-scissor-tag/>)

### 11. Dragon’s Tail

*A very long creature with a very short plan.*

Playgrounds & camps worldwide · 6–12 people · 5–10 minutes · Suggested age 6+ · medium space · Energy 2/3

**Also known as:** Catch the Dragon’s Tail

**Equipment:** None

One group becomes one gloriously uncoordinated animal. Its head wants to reach its tail, while the middle has to negotiate every turn; the funniest moment is realising you are helping both sides.

**Set up:** Form a line with light hands on the hips or shoulders ahead, where comfortable. Front player is head; last player is tail.

1. Move at walking pace while staying connected.
2. The head tries to gently tag the tail; the tail tries to stay out of reach.
3. If the line breaks, everyone pauses, reconnects and starts again.
4. When the tail is tagged, stop. The head moves to the back and becomes the new tail.
5. Continue until everyone has had a turn at the front.

**How it ends:** Complete the rotation together; there is no individual elimination.

**The twist:** Your pursuer and your escape route are attached to you.

**Mix it up:** Hosting adaptation: use short groups of six so everyone reaches the head more often.

**Make it work:** No yanking, tight whips or running. Let go immediately if anybody loses balance.

**Starter rules:** Uses the head-catching-its-own-tail form; pause-on-break is an added hosting rule.

**Sources:**

- [City of Toronto · Dragon’s Tail Tag](<https://www.toronto.ca/community-people/health-wellness-care/health-programs-advice/physical-activity/outdoors-the-ultimate-playground/games/go-play-tag/>)

### 12. Capture the Flag

*Someone has to make the impossible run.*

Playgrounds & camps worldwide · 10–30 people · 10–20 minutes · Suggested age 8+ · large space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** CTF

**Equipment:** 2 flags or cloths, Boundary markers

One player draws attention, another slips through, and somebody takes a detour to rescue a teammate. The best run often belongs to the person nobody noticed until the flag was already moving.

**Set up:** Divide a field in half. Each team gets a visible flag zone and a holding zone; establish space around both for defenders to respect.

1. Enter the opposing half to steal its flag and carry it across the centre line.
2. On the opponents’ half, a gentle tag sends you to their holding zone. You are safe on your own half.
3. If tagged carrying the flag, return it to its zone and go to the holding area.
4. A free teammate can tag one held player to rescue them; both walk safely home before rejoining.
5. A successful flag return scores one point. Reset flags and all players; play first to three.

**How it ends:** First team to return the opposing flag three times wins this starter match.

**The twist:** The best attack may begin with a rescue.

**Mix it up:** Use the documented mass-rescue variant: reaching the holding zone frees the whole group.

**Make it work:** No tackling or grabbing flags from hands. Keep defenders back from flag and holding zones.

**Starter rules:** First-to-three scoring is a hosting choice; flag-zone and rescue rules vary locally.

**Sources:**

- [Playworks · Capture the Flag](<https://www.playworks.org/game-library/capture-the-flag/>)

## Dodge & rescue

Change what being “out” means. This chapter includes rescues, special roles and target-only relatives as well as elimination games.

<a id="hoop-rescue"></a>

### 13. Hoop Rescue Dodgeball

*One impossible basket. Everybody comes back.*

Playgrounds & camps worldwide · 10–30 people · 8–15 minutes · Suggested age 8+ · large space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** Basketball Rescue Dodgeball, Prison Break Dodgeball

**Equipment:** Soft foam balls, Basketball hoop, Floor markers

The team is nearly gone. One player looks past every defender and sends the ball toward the hoop. A single basket can erase the entire collapse: suddenly all those waiting teammates are back.

**Set up:** Divide a basketball court into two halves. Mark a floor-level rescue zone behind each team for caught opponents; start with equal balls.

1. Stay in your half. A direct ball hit below the waist sends you to the rescue zone behind your opponents. Bounced hits do not count.
2. Waiting players can catch a pass from their own team to earn a free walk home.
3. Active players may catch incoming throws to stay safe; this starter version adds no catch bonus.
4. Score in the opponents’ basket without crossing centre and your entire waiting team returns.
5. Win a round by sending all opponents to the rescue zone. Reset everyone and rotate starting sides.

**How it ends:** Send every opponent to the rescue zone before they engineer a comeback.

**The twist:** The basketball hoop becomes a team-wide undo button.

**Mix it up:** Hosting adaptation: lower the basket or move a marked shooting line closer for mixed abilities.

**Make it work:** Use floor zones rather than standing on benches. Pause throws while players cross home; no hard or high throws.

**Starter rules:** Based on documented basket rescue; benches and barricades are omitted and catching is simplified.

**Sources:**

- [Elementary PE Games · Barricade Bench Dodgeball](<https://www.pegames.org/full-length/barricade-bench-dodgeball>)

### 14. Noodle Keeper Dodgeball

*A goalkeeper. A pool noodle. Unreasonable confidence.*

Playgrounds & camps worldwide · 8–24 people · 8–15 minutes · Suggested age 8+ · large space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** Goalkeeper Dodgeball

**Equipment:** Soft foam balls, 2 pool noodles, Cones for 2 goals

Now there is something else to defend. A goalkeeper with a ridiculous foam extension can turn a perfect shot away, while the rest of the team decides whether to target players or the open goal.

**Set up:** Make two teams on opposite halves, with a wide cone goal at each end. Choose a keeper per team and mark a small keeper area.

1. Stay in your half. Direct hits below the waist put players out until the next point; bounced hits do not count.
2. A clean catch puts the thrower out. Keepers follow the same body-hit rule.
3. Keepers can block with a noodle held low; noodle contact alone does not put them out.
4. A rolled or thrown ball passing through the opposing goal below knee height scores one point. Eliminating the whole opposing team also scores one.
5. After a point, all players return, balls are shared evenly and keepers rotate. First to three wins.

**How it ends:** Score three points through goals or full-team eliminations.

**The twist:** Dodgeball suddenly has a goal and a specialist defender.

**Mix it up:** House option: remove player eliminations and score only goals for continuous play.

**Make it work:** Use cones, not rigid goalposts. Keep noodles below waist height; never swing at people.

**House adaptation:** A user-inspired house game designed for this atlas; the source supports noodle use, not this exact goal ruleset.

**Sources:**

- [S&S Worldwide · Dodgeball variations, including noodle blocking](<https://www.ssww.com/blog/dodgeball-game-variations-for-summer-camp-recreation/>)

### 15. Medic Dodgeball

*Find the healer. Protect the comeback.*

Playgrounds & camps worldwide · 10–30 people · 5–15 minutes · Suggested age 8+ · large space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** Doctor Dodgeball

**Equipment:** Soft foam balls, Boundary markers

Getting hit changes your role instead of ending your game. A secret medic can restore movement, but every rescue reveals a little more about who the other team should be watching.

**Set up:** Split a rectangle into two team halves. Each team secretly identifies one medic to the host; distribute balls evenly.

1. Stay in your half. A direct hit below the waist or a dropped catch makes you stationary; bounced hits do not count.
2. Catching a live throw makes the thrower stationary instead.
3. Stationary players stay at their spot but may still catch and throw.
4. Only a moving medic can restore a teammate with a gentle shoulder tap. Once hit, the medic cannot heal anyone, including themselves.
5. The round ends when an entire team is stationary, or after five minutes. Reset and choose new medics.

**How it ends:** Make every opponent stationary; if time expires, call a draw and rotate medics.

**The twist:** Your most valuable player is revealed by helping others.

**Mix it up:** Hosting adaptation: identify medics with a bib for younger groups who need clearer roles.

**Make it work:** Offer a standing stationary pose instead of kneeling. Aim low and give stationary players breathing room.

**Starter rules:** Uses the version where stationary players may still throw; a timed draw is our hosting choice.

**Sources:**

- [Playworks · Medic Dodgeball](<https://www.playworks.org/game-library/medic-dodgeball/>)

### 16. Gaga

*All the drama happens below the knees.*

Playgrounds & camps worldwide · 6–18 people · 3–8 minutes · Suggested age 8+ · medium space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** Ga-Ga Ball, Octoball

**Equipment:** Soft foam ball, Safe Gaga pit or low-sided enclosure

A rebound you barely noticed becomes the next threat. Inside a small shared arena, everyone slaps the same ball low and keeps their feet moving; even a self-inflicted bounce can end your round.

**Set up:** Use a safe pit with clear entry and exit. Everyone starts with a hand on the wall; a host drops the ball in the centre.

1. Call “ga, ga, go” on three bounces; play begins on “go”.
2. Strike the ball with an open hand. Do not carry, throw or kick it.
3. A ball touching you below the knee puts you out, even from your own hit. Exit carefully.
4. A clean airborne catch puts the last hitter out. If the ball leaves the pit, its last toucher is out; pause, retrieve and restart with three bounces.
5. The last player in wins; everyone returns for the next short round.

**How it ends:** Be the final player in the pit.

**The twist:** The walls keep yesterday’s shot alive as tomorrow’s problem.

**Mix it up:** Common local option: forbid consecutive touches until the ball hits a wall or another player.

**Make it work:** Use a soft ball and uncrowded pit. Do not climb or sit on the walls; adapt the target zone with the group.

**Starter rules:** Combines the Gaga Center’s below-knee game with a documented ball-out rule; double-touch rules differ by venue.

**Sources:**

- [The Gaga Center · Rules of Gaga Ball](<https://www.gagacenter.com/about/what-is-gaga/>)
- [Medford Public Schools · Gaga Pit Rules](<https://www.mps02155.org/students-families/gaga-pit-rules>)

### 17. Four-Corner Dodgeball

*Three opponents. Nowhere to hide your weak side.*

Playgrounds & camps worldwide · 12–32 people · 5–12 minutes · Suggested age 8+ · large space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** 4-Way Dodgeball

**Equipment:** Soft foam balls, Court markers

A front line is comforting until attacks can come from three directions. Four teams share a court, making every safe-looking corner part of somebody else’s shooting angle.

**Set up:** Divide a court into four equal squares. Put one team and an equal number of soft balls in each square.

1. Stay within your square and throw only below opponents’ waists.
2. A direct live hit puts you out; a floor bounce makes the ball dead.
3. Catch a live throw and its thrower is out. No catch rescues in this starter round.
4. Out players wait beyond the court; a team with nobody left is eliminated.
5. The last team with active players wins. At three minutes, stop and reset everyone if no team has won.

**How it ends:** Be the last team with players in the court, or draw at the time limit.

**The twist:** Defending one boundary leaves two others exposed.

**Mix it up:** Hosting adaptation: freeze all balls, rotate teams one square, then restart on the host’s signal.

**Make it work:** Split large groups across courts. No throwing into waiting areas; leave a buffer between players and walls.

**Starter rules:** Uses four-team elimination; rotation is an optional stopped-play adaptation.

**Sources:**

- [The Summer Camp Source · 4 Way Dodgeball](<https://thesummercampsource.com/4-way-dodgeball/>)

### 18. Two-Pin Dodgeball

*Two targets. Two chances to turn everything around.*

Playgrounds & camps worldwide · 8–24 people · 5–12 minutes · Suggested age 8+ · large space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** Two Pin Dodgeball

**Equipment:** Soft foam balls, 4 light plastic pins, Boundary markers

Players can dodge, but the pins cannot. Suddenly defenders have to commit to a place, and a desperate shot at a distant target can both revive your whole team and put victory within reach.

**Set up:** Split the court into halves. Each team places two light pins near its back line and starts with equal balls.

1. Remain in your half. Direct hits below the waist eliminate; bounced hits do not count.
2. A clean catch eliminates the thrower but does not rescue a teammate.
3. Knock over an opposing pin with a ball and every eliminated player on your team returns.
4. Leave knocked-down pins down. Do not move or hold your own pins during play.
5. Knock both opposing pins down, or eliminate all opposing players, to win. Reset pins and players for the next round.

**How it ends:** Win through either two fallen pins or one fully eliminated team.

**The twist:** A target doubles as a life refill.

**Mix it up:** House option: use only target scoring, with no player elimination, for a gentler round.

**Make it work:** Use lightweight pins and soft balls. Defend from in front without standing on or grabbing the pin.

**Starter rules:** This documented form rescues players only through pins, not catches.

**Sources:**

- [The Summer Camp Source · 2 Pin Dodgeball](<https://thesummercampsource.com/2-pin-dodgeball/>)

### 19. Castleball

*Build it. Defend it. Try not to love it too much.*

Playgrounds & camps worldwide · 8–30 people · 8–15 minutes · Suggested age 8+ · large space · Energy 2/3

**Also known as:** Castle Ball, Hula-Hoop Castleball

**Equipment:** 24 sturdy hula hoops, Soft foam balls

The target is something your team just built. Attackers, blockers and hurried rebuilders are all useful, so the game keeps generating small jobs even when throwing is not your favourite part.

**Set up:** Make two teams on separate halves. Each builds two castles: one hoop flat, four leaning sides, one resting on top.

1. Practise making stable castles before adding balls.
2. On the signal, throw from your half to knock down opposing castles.
3. Players may block or catch, but never cross the centre. Hits on players do not eliminate them.
4. Each toppled castle scores one for the attacking team. Rebuild fallen castles while play continues.
5. Stop after five minutes, compare scores and rebuild for another round.

**How it ends:** Most castles toppled during the timed round wins.

**The twist:** Construction is an active role in the middle of the match.

**Mix it up:** Hosting adaptation: begin with one castle per team and pause play for rebuilds.

**Make it work:** Keep castles away from walls. Aim at structures, use soft balls, and protect a working rebuilder’s space.

**Starter rules:** This is target-only Castleball; player-elimination versions also exist.

- [PHYSEDGAMES · Castleball](<https://physedgames.com/castleball/>)

### 20. Protect the President

*The strongest thrower may not be your most important player.*

Playgrounds & camps worldwide · 8–24 people · 5–12 minutes · Suggested age 8+ · large space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** VIP Dodgeball

**Equipment:** Soft foam balls, 2 identifying bibs

Your team can be winning everywhere and still lose in one moment. The protected player changes everyone’s priorities: a good block, a quiet position and a timely rescue become as valuable as a throw.

**Set up:** Make two teams on opposite court halves. Each identifies a president who dodges but does not throw. Rotate this role every round.

1. Stay on your half and throw below the waist. A direct live hit eliminates a guard; a bounced ball is dead.
2. Guards can catch incoming balls: the thrower goes out and one waiting teammate returns.
3. Guards defend without grabbing or surrounding the president with contact. The president may move within their half.
4. A legal hit on the opposing president immediately wins the round.
5. After three minutes without a winner, draw. Reset all players and choose new presidents.

**How it ends:** Land a legal below-waist hit on the opposing president.

**The twist:** One player’s survival matters more than the scoreboard.

**Mix it up:** Hosting adaptation: keep the president secret, with the host checking the choice before play.

**Make it work:** Use soft balls; no diving in front of shots or close-range volleys. Allow anyone to decline the president role.

**Starter rules:** Visible, non-throwing presidents and a three-minute draw are hosting adaptations.

**Sources:**

- [The Summer Camp Source · Protect the President](<https://thesummercampsource.com/protect-the-president/>)

### 21. Nationball

*Out of the court. Still very much in the game.*

Playgrounds & camps worldwide · 10–30 people · 5–12 minutes · Suggested age 8+ · large space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** Nation Ball, Outside Dodgeball

**Equipment:** Soft foam balls, Boundary markers

Eliminating an opponent opens a new threat behind you. Players outside the court can still attack and pass, turning a familiar two-sided game into a problem of front, back and everywhere between.

**Set up:** Make two team halves. Give each team an outside lane behind its opponents, separated from the active court by a two-metre no-throw buffer.

1. Start with equal balls. From your half, throw below opposing players’ waists.
2. A direct hit sends a player outside to their team’s assigned lane; floor-bounced hits do not count.
3. Outside players can collect loose balls, pass to teammates and throw at active opponents. They stay outside for the round.
4. Active players may catch live throws to make them safe; this starter version has no catch bonus or revival.
5. When one team has nobody inside, the other wins. Reset everyone and switch starting sides.

**How it ends:** Leave the opposing team with no active players inside the court.

**The twist:** Elimination changes your position rather than ending your participation.

**Mix it up:** Hosting adaptation: outside players pass only, reducing the number of attack directions.

**Make it work:** Use a clear separation between inside players and outside throwers. No shots from point-blank range.

**Starter rules:** Uses the no-return outside-player version; rear lanes, a buffer and simplified catches are hosting adaptations.

**Sources:**

- [The Summer Camp Source · Nation Ball](<https://thesummercampsource.com/nation-ball/>)

### 22. Sprout Ball

*Remember who got you. Their downfall is your comeback.*

Playgrounds & camps worldwide · 8–24 people · 5–12 minutes · Suggested age 8+ · large space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** Resurrection Dodgeball, Sproutball

**Equipment:** Soft foam balls

The player who put you out is now the most interesting person on the court. As soon as they fall, you return; one well-placed shot can suddenly bring a whole cluster of rivals back.

**Set up:** Use one open, bounded space with no teams. Spread the players out and start with two or three soft balls.

1. A player holding a ball gets at most three steps and five seconds to throw below another player’s waist.
2. A direct hit or dropped catch makes you a stationary “seed” who cannot handle balls; floor-bounced hits do not count.
3. Catch a live throw and its thrower becomes a seed instead.
4. Remember the person who got you: when they become a seed, you immediately return as a moving sprout.
5. Finish when only one sprout remains or the timer ends. Reset all seeds for the next round.

**How it ends:** Be the last moving sprout; a timed round may finish without a sole winner.

**The twist:** Every elimination leaves a chain of possible revivals.

**Mix it up:** Documented option: seeds can reach for nearby balls and throw to free themselves.

**Make it work:** Use a standing seed pose if kneeling is unsuitable. Keep shots low and leave stationary players space.

**Starter rules:** The eliminator-linked return is central; this starter omits the source’s three-bounce opening.

**Sources:**

- [Playworks · Sprout Ball](<https://www.playworks.org/game-library/sprout-ball/>)

### 23. Money Ball Dodgeball

*One ball. Two ways to turn the game.*

Playgrounds & camps worldwide · 8–24 people · 5–12 minutes · Suggested age 8+ · large space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** Power Ball Dodgeball

**Equipment:** Soft foam balls, 1 larger soft ball in a distinct colour

One ball carries completely different stakes. A hit brings somebody back; a catch ends the entire round. Both teams watch it nervously, because hanging onto it is not an option.

**Set up:** Divide a court into team halves. Identify the larger, soft money ball clearly and agree a five-second possession limit.

1. Stay in your half. Direct below-waist hits eliminate players; bounced hits do not count.
2. Ordinary-ball catches eliminate the thrower but never revive teammates.
3. A team holding the money ball must throw it across centre within five seconds; deliberately rolling it loses that thrower.
4. A legal money-ball hit eliminates its target and returns one waiting player from the throwing team.
5. Catch a live money-ball throw to win immediately. Eliminating every opponent also wins; reset for another round.

**How it ends:** Catch the money ball or eliminate the whole opposing team.

**The twist:** The strongest comeback tool is also an instant-loss risk.

**Mix it up:** House option: a money-ball catch rescues the whole team instead of ending the game.

**Make it work:** The special ball must still be soft and light. No hard throws or aiming above the waist.

**Starter rules:** Five-second possession is a hosting addition; the rescue and instant-catch win are documented.

**Sources:**

- [The Summer Camp Source · Money Ball Dodgeball](<https://thesummercampsource.com/money-ball-dodgeball/>)

### 24. Rollerball

*Dodgeball gets down to earth.*

Playgrounds & camps worldwide · 4–12 people · 3–8 minutes · Suggested age 5+ · small space · Energy 2/3

**Also known as:** Rolling Dodgeball

**Equipment:** 1 soft ball, Floor markers

With one rolling ball and one clear direction of attack, the game becomes wonderfully readable. A small sidestep matters more than a powerful throw, making it an easy first taste of dodging.

**Set up:** Mark a wide dodging strip and a separate rolling line a few metres away. Choose one roller; everyone else starts in the strip.

1. The roller sends the ball along the floor toward the dodgers’ feet.
2. Dodgers stay inside their strip and move sideways to avoid contact.
3. A below-waist rolling hit sends a dodger to the waiting area for this short round. High throws do not count.
4. Pause while the ball is retrieved; restart only when everybody is ready.
5. The last dodger becomes the next roller. All players return for the new round.

**How it ends:** Be the last dodger; then take a turn as roller.

**The twist:** One low ball makes tiny changes of direction matter.

**Mix it up:** Hosting adaptation: give everyone three lives so early hits do not end their turn immediately.

**Make it work:** Use floor markings instead of lining bodies directly against a wall. Roll gently; never kick or throw the ball.

**Starter rules:** Based on Playworks’ rolling game; a marked strip replaces the wall and retrieval is paused.

**Sources:**

- [Playworks · Rollerball](<https://www.playworks.org/game-library/rollerball/>)

### 25. Flunkyball · token edition

*A knocked bottle starts a race against the reset.*

Germany / festival-game adaptation · 6–16 people · 10–20 minutes · Suggested age 8+ · medium space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** Flunky Ball, Bierball, Flankyball

**Equipment:** Soft ball, Plastic bottle partly filled with water, 20 large tokens, 2 bowls, Cones

One accurate throw starts two different races: one team scores while the other rebuilds the field. This version keeps Flunkyball’s frantic stop-call rhythm and swaps the usual adult drinking for tokens.

**Set up:** Two teams face each other behind lines about five metres from a centre bottle. Give each team ten tokens and a bowl one metre behind its line.

1. Teams alternate one throw at the bottle, rotating throwers.
2. When it falls, the throwing team starts a relay: one player at a time carries one token to their bowl, returns and tags the next.
3. The other team resets the bottle, retrieves the ball and gets everyone back behind its line.
4. Only then call “Stop!” No more tokens may enter the bowl; a carried token returns to the supply.
5. Swap throwing teams after every attempt. A miss starts no relay.

**How it ends:** First team to bank all ten tokens wins after the stop call.

**The twist:** Your target is also your opponent’s reset button.

**Mix it up:** Access adaptation: pass tokens along a seated line instead of running the relay.

**Make it work:** Use plastic and a soft ball; keep relay lanes behind the teams, away from the retrieval route.

**House adaptation:** Flunkyball is commonly an adult drinking game. Token-relay scoring here is a designed alcohol-free adaptation.

**Sources:**

- [Flunkyball Verband Dortmund — Regelwerk](<https://www.flunken.de/regelwerk>)

### 26. Kubb

*Knock down a block. Create tomorrow’s problem.*

Sweden / international lawn play · 2–12 people · 20–40 minutes · Suggested age 8+ · medium space · Energy 1/3

**Also known as:** Viking chess

**Equipment:** Kubb set, Boundary markers

Fallen blocks return as new targets in the middle of the court. Every successful attack changes the next turn, until one team earns a shot at the king that has been waiting all along.

**Set up:** Mark a roughly 5 × 8 metre rectangle. Put five kubbs on each baseline and the king in the centre. Divide six batons between teammates.

1. Throw batons underarm, end over end, at opposing kubbs. For a simple opening, give each team six throws per turn.
2. Before their reply, opponents toss fallen kubbs into your half; stand them where they land as field kubbs.
3. Clear all field kubbs before attacking baseline kubbs. If any remain, opponents may throw from the line of the surviving field kubb closest to the centre.
4. Re-throw an out-of-bounds field kubb once; a second miss lets opponents place it, at least a baton-length from king and boundaries.
5. After clearing every opposing kubb, shoot at the king from your original baseline. Toppling it early loses immediately.

**How it ends:** Clear the opposing kubbs, then topple the king legally.

**The twist:** A successful hit sends its target back into the game.

**Mix it up:** Use the documented 2–4–6 baton opening when both teams know the basics.

**Make it work:** Everyone stands clear of the target half; collect wooden pieces only when throwing stops.

**Starter rules:** Casual six-baton opening; competition opening sequences and details vary by ruleset.

**Sources:**

- [Kubb World — Game rules](<https://www.kubb.world/game-rules>)
- [USA Kubb — Rules and tips](<https://www.usakubb.org/rules>)

### 27. Mölkky

*One pin means its number. Many pins mean their count.*

Finland · 2–8 people · 15–30 minutes · Suggested age 7+ · medium space · Energy 1/3

**Also known as:** Molky, Molkky

**Equipment:** Mölkky set, Score sheet

The pins spread wider after every throw, turning a simple skittle game into a moving arithmetic puzzle. You might need the lonely twelve, or deliberately aim for two low-value pins instead.

**Set up:** Arrange the twelve numbered pins in the set’s starting cluster. Mark a throwing line about 3.5 metres away and choose turn order.

1. Throw the wooden throwing pin underarm to knock down numbered pins.
2. If exactly one falls fully, score its number. If several fall, score the number of fallen pins.
3. Stand them upright where they fell, so the target pattern gradually spreads.
4. Take turns adding scores. Going above 50 resets your total to 25; three consecutive scoreless throws eliminate you.
5. Keep playing until someone reaches exactly 50.

**How it ends:** Exactly 50 wins. House fallback: if eliminations leave one player, they win; if none remain, share the result.

**The twist:** Better aim can mean knocking over fewer pins.

**Mix it up:** Intro adaptation: after three misses, lose five points instead of leaving the game.

**Make it work:** Wait behind the throwing line and check the entire spread before each throw.

**Documented game:** These are the maker’s basic rules; the no-elimination option is an adaptation.

**Sources:**

- [Mölkky Oy — Rules](<https://www.molkky.com/game/rules/>)

### 28. Pétanque

*A quiet little throw can completely change the picture.*

France · 2–6 people · 15–30 minutes · Suggested age 7+ · medium space · Energy 1/3

**Also known as:** Petanque, Boules

**Equipment:** Boules, Small jack, Throwing circle, Measuring tape

Every ball is both a possible point and a piece of terrain. Sometimes you roll close; sometimes you knock an opponent away. The team that is losing the current position gets to keep trying.

**Set up:** Choose level gravel or firm ground. Play singles or pairs with three boules per player, or triples with two. Mark a shared throwing circle.

1. Throw the small jack 6–10 metres away, then send the first boule towards it.
2. The other team throws until it becomes closest or uses all its boules.
3. Continue with whichever team is not closest. Keep both feet grounded inside the circle while throwing.
4. After all balls are played, only the closer team scores: one for each boule nearer than the opponent’s nearest.
5. The scoring team starts the next end from a circle near the previous jack.

**How it ends:** First team to 13 points wins.

**The twist:** You choose between building a better position and destroying theirs.

**Mix it up:** Access adaptation: use soft indoor boules and shorter measured distances.

**Make it work:** Keep people out of the landing area; metal boules are heavy even on gentle throws.

**Starter rules:** A basic introduction; dead-jack situations and measurement disputes are covered by the full rules.

**Sources:**

- [Federation of Pétanque USA — How to play](<https://www.usapetanque.org/how-to-play-peacutetanque.html>)

### 29. Bocce

*Read the ground. Roll a better answer.*

Italy / international lawn play · 2–8 people · 15–30 minutes · Suggested age 6+ · medium space · Energy 1/3

**Also known as:** Boccia lawn game

**Equipment:** 8 bocce balls, Pallino target ball, Boundary markers

A slow ball can be the boldest move on the court. Bocce rewards reading tiny slopes, using an opponent’s ball as a stop, and leaving your next teammate something useful to build on.

**Set up:** Mark a long, narrow lane. Split eight balls equally between two teams and choose who throws the small pallino first.

1. Roll the pallino into the far half, then roll your first ball near it.
2. The team farther from the pallino keeps playing until it gets closer or runs out of balls.
3. When one team has no balls left, the other rolls its remaining balls. Contact between balls can change the result.
4. At the end, the closest team scores one for each ball nearer than the opponents’ closest.
5. The scoring team begins the next end, reversing the direction of play.

**How it ends:** First to 12 points wins this starter game.

**The twist:** The surface is part of your strategy.

**Mix it up:** Access adaptation: let a player use a rolling ramp with their chosen direction.

**Make it work:** Choose balls players can control; retrieve only after the end is scored.

**Starter rules:** Open-bocce scoring on a casual lane; regulation courts add specific wall and foul-line rules.

**Sources:**

- [United States Bocce Federation — Open rules](<https://usbf.us/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/USBF-Open-Rules_03-23-23.pdf>)

### 30. Cornhole

*Slide, block, or drop straight through.*

United States · 2–4 people · 10–25 minutes · Suggested age 6+ · medium space · Energy 1/3

**Also known as:** Beanbag toss

**Equipment:** 2 cornhole boards, 8 beanbags

A bag in the way might be a mistake, a blockade or a ramp. Because scores cancel at the end of the round, your last throw can erase an opponent’s brilliant opening.

**Set up:** Face two boards towards each other. Standard adult spacing is about 8.2 metres; bring them closer for beginners. Use four bags per side.

1. Opponents alternate underarm throws at the far board until each has thrown four bags.
2. A bag through the hole scores three; a bag resting on the board scores one. Ground-first bags score zero and are removed.
3. Wait until all bags settle, then subtract the lower round score from the higher. Only the difference is added.
4. Throw from the other end next round; the side that scored throws first. At a tied round, keep the previous order.

**How it ends:** First to 21 or more at the end of a round wins; there is no bust penalty.

**The twist:** A last bag can turn a whole round into zero.

**Mix it up:** Learning adaptation: start closer and gradually move back after successful rounds.

**Make it work:** Keep the lane clear and use beanbags light enough for every player.

**Documented game:** Core American Cornhole Association scoring; shortened distances are a casual adjustment.

**Sources:**

- [American Cornhole Association — Official rules](<https://www.playcornhole.org/pages/rules>)

### 31. KanJam

*Your partner is the last-metre flight controller.*

United States · 4 people · 10–20 minutes · Suggested age 8+ · medium space · Energy 1/3

**Also known as:** Kan Jam

**Equipment:** Flying disc, 2 KanJam goals

The throw is only the beginning. Your teammate gets a single chance to redirect the disc, turning a near miss into a hit or a glorious downward dunk. Precision and improvisation share the same point.

**Set up:** Set two goals facing each other, normally about 15 metres apart; shorten for beginners. Each pair has one teammate at each end.

1. Throw from behind your goal towards your partner’s goal. They may redirect once, without catching or carrying.
2. A deflected outside hit scores one; an unassisted outside hit scores two; a deflection inside scores three.
3. Partners each throw once per team turn, then the other pair takes its turn. A ground-first throw scores zero.
4. Reach exactly 21; if a throw would exceed it, subtract that throw’s value instead. Complete equal team turns.
5. An unassisted disc staying inside through the slot or top wins immediately. Otherwise, ties use equal overtime turns.

**How it ends:** Exactly 21 after equal turns, or an instant-win direct entry.

**The twist:** The best shot may need someone else to finish it.

**Mix it up:** Intro adaptation: shorten the gap until each player can reach the goal comfortably.

**Make it work:** Use controlled throws and open-hand deflections; keep spectators behind the goals.

**Starter rules:** Scoring follows KanJam’s rules; the linked guides explain complete turns, direct entries and overtime.

**Sources:**

- [KanJam — How to play](<https://www.kanjam.com/pages/how-to-play>)

### 32. Ladder toss

*Aim for a rung. Hope it stays there.*

United States / garden play · 2–4 people · 10–20 minutes · Suggested age 7+ · small space · Energy 1/3

**Also known as:** Ladder golf, Bolo toss

**Equipment:** Ladder-toss frame, 6 soft bolas

Two balls tied together spin through the air and wrap around a rung. But a point is not safe yet: the next throw can shake your perfect landing straight back onto the grass.

**Set up:** Stand a three-rung frame on level ground and mark a comfortable throwing line. Give each side three differently coloured bolas.

1. Each side tosses all three bolas underarm, then the other side takes its turn.
2. Leave landed bolas in place until the whole round ends; knocked-off bolas no longer score.
3. Score one for the bottom rung, two for the middle and three for the top.
4. For this simple version, use no bonuses or cancellation. Add each side’s remaining points, then clear the frame.
5. Reach exactly 21; if your round would exceed 21, discard that round’s points. Alternate which side starts.

**How it ends:** First to exactly 21 after a complete round wins; play another round if both reach it.

**The twist:** Points can literally fall off the scoreboard.

**Mix it up:** Documented bonus option: add one for all three on one rung, or one on each rung.

**Make it work:** Use soft bolas, not golf balls, for younger groups; nobody stands near the frame during throws.

**Starter rules:** This uses the university guide’s rung values and bust rule, with explicit simple turn order and no bonuses.

**Sources:**

- [CSU Channel Islands — Basic ladder golf rules](<https://www.csuci.edu/student-life/recreation/ladder-golf-rules.html>)

### 33. Washer toss

*A small ring. A smaller target. A very loud success.*

North America · 2–4 people · 10–20 minutes · Suggested age 8+ · small space · Energy 1/3

**Also known as:** Washers

**Equipment:** 2 target boxes with centre cups, 6 rubber washers

The satisfying part is the tiny landing zone. A gentle toss can bounce into the box or disappear into the centre cup, and one good round can cancel every point your opponent thought they had.

**Set up:** Place two cup-in-box targets a few metres apart. Play singles or pairs, with teammates at opposite ends. Give each side three washers.

1. Alternate tossing washers into the far box until all six have been thrown.
2. A washer in the centre cup scores three; elsewhere inside the box scores one. Outside scores zero.
3. Subtract the lower round score from the higher; only the difference is added to the leading side.
4. Collect washers and play back towards the other box. The side that scored starts the next round.

**How it ends:** First to 21 or more at the end of a round wins.

**The twist:** The safe one-point box surrounds the tempting three-point cup.

**Mix it up:** Precision adaptation: shrink the centre cup once everyone is landing in the box.

**Make it work:** Use large rubber washers for casual play and keep feet clear of the targets.

**Starter rules:** Box-and-cup format; washer games have many regional scores. We omit edge instant-wins, skunks and exact-21 variants.

**Sources:**

- [Gather Together Games — Washer toss](<https://gathertogethergames.com/washer-toss>)

### 34. Golf croquet

*Everyone wants the same hoop. Only one gets it.*

England / international croquet · 2–4 people · 20–45 minutes · Suggested age 8+ · medium space · Energy 1/3

**Also known as:** Golf Croquet

**Equipment:** 4 croquet balls, Mallets, 6 hoops, Centre peg

One stroke per turn makes this a patient tactical duel. You can line up your next shot, block a rival or knock their ball away, but while you prepare, someone else may simply score.

**Set up:** Use a croquet lawn layout with six hoops. Blue and black face red and yellow. Start near corner four; review the source’s route diagram together.

1. Take one stroke in the fixed order blue, red, black, yellow; each player controls their assigned colour or colours.
2. Everyone contests the same current hoop from its marked playing side.
3. The first ball completely through it scores one for its side. All players then contest the next hoop.
4. Hitting another ball gives no extra stroke. If a ball leaves the lawn, replace it on the boundary where it crossed.
5. Follow the numbered twelve-hoop route in the source diagram, keeping balls where they finish. If the score reaches 6–6, contest hoop three again.

**How it ends:** First side to seven hoop points wins.

**The twist:** You can move an opponent’s ball without ever earning an extra turn.

**Mix it up:** Short-session adaptation: play seven contested hoops, first side to four.

**Make it work:** Keep mallet swings low and wait well clear of the striker; wider hoops make an easier introduction.

**Starter rules:** Golf croquet, not association croquet; advanced offside and fault rules remain in the full guide.

**Sources:**

- [Croquet England — Play golf croquet](<https://www.croquetengland.org.uk/play-golf-croquet/>)

### 35. Disc golf

*Turn the park into a course, one flight at a time.*

United States / international disc sport · 2–6 people · 20–45 minutes · Suggested age 8+ · large space · Energy 1/3

**Also known as:** Frisbee golf

**Equipment:** Flying discs, Disc-golf baskets or agreed targets, Score sheet

The pleasure is choosing a route: a direct throw through a gap or a gentle curve around it. Every landing becomes the start of the next problem, and a short putt can matter as much as a huge drive.

**Set up:** Use an established course, or agree a short course of safe basket targets on permitted open ground. Pick tees and boundaries before throwing.

1. Everyone throws from the marked tee, one at a time.
2. Play each next throw from where your previous disc stopped. The farthest player usually throws next.
3. Count every throw until the disc finishes in the basket. A target-course adaptation may instead require touching a named target.
4. For this starter round, an out-of-bounds throw adds one penalty; resume just inside where it last crossed out.
5. Record the total for the hole, then move together to the next tee.

**How it ends:** Lowest total throw count across the agreed course wins.

**The twist:** Your previous mistake becomes the next place you have to solve.

**Mix it up:** Doubles adaptation: both teammates throw, choose the better landing and both continue from there.

**Make it work:** Never throw towards people or animals; wait for the entire landing area to clear.

**Starter rules:** Core disc-golf format; use the actual course’s signs and current rules for formal rounds.

**Sources:**

- [PDGA — Description of the game](<https://www.pdga.com/rules/official-rules-disc-golf/800>)

### 36. Gorodki

*Demolish a tiny wooden skyline.*

Russia / Eastern Europe · 2–8 people · 15–25 minutes · Suggested age 10+ · medium space · Energy 1/3

**Also known as:** Городки

**Equipment:** 5 soft cylindrical blocks, Soft throwing baton, Square boundary markers

The target is a little sculpture made from five blocks. Knocking it down is not enough: every piece must leave the square. Each new shape asks for a different angle of demolition.

**Set up:** Mark a target square and a throwing line. Build the same five-block figure for each side; use soft practice pieces and generous clear space.

1. Take turns with two underarm baton throws at the figure.
2. A block counts only when it is completely outside the target square.
3. Leave remaining pieces where they stop. Continue until all five have cleared.
4. Record the throws used, rebuild for the next player or team, and repeat with the next agreed shape.
5. For this short game, use three figures: a straight row, a cross and a five-block well.

**How it ends:** Fewest total throws across the three figures wins.

**The twist:** A collapsed target can still refuse to leave.

**Mix it up:** Documented informal option: give equal limited throws and compare blocks cleared instead.

**Make it work:** Use soft equipment for introductions; traditional heavy batons require a dedicated protected throwing area.

**House adaptation:** A soft-equipment three-figure introduction; formal gorodki uses a prescribed series of figures and distances.

### 37. Kho Kho

*You can pass the chase.*

India / South Asia · 12–18 people · 10–20 minutes · Suggested age 8+ · large space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** Kho-kho

**Equipment:** Cones

The fastest route to a runner might be through a teammate. Seated chasers become switches in a living circuit, handing over pursuit the moment a runner changes sides.

**Set up:** Mark a rectangle, a central lane and an end cone at each end. Divide teams equally; all but one chaser sit along the lane facing alternate sides.

1. Send three runners into the court. One active chaser begins beyond an end cone.
2. Runners may change direction and cross the lane. The chaser cannot cross it or reverse direction except around an end cone.
3. Tap a seated teammate’s back and call “kho”; sit in their place as they begin chasing on the side they face.
4. Each gentle tag scores one. Tagged runners leave; send the next three when a batch is out. Recycle batches if needed.
5. After five minutes, swap chasing and running. Give both teams equal turns.

**How it ends:** Most tags after both teams have chased wins; a tie stands.

**The twist:** Your position and facing direction can matter more than your speed.

**Mix it up:** Intro adaptation: walk through one practice chase before running.

**Make it work:** Leave generous gaps between seated players; runners go around people, never over them.

**Starter rules:** Shorter innings and flexible team sizes; the source explains the full nine-a-side game.

**Sources:**

- [Tamil Nadu Kho Kho Association — Game explanation](<https://tamilnadukhokhoassociation.com/game-explanation/>)

### 38. Kabaddi · tag edition

*One raider. An entire half full of trouble.*

South Asia / school adaptation · 6–14 people · 10–15 minutes · Suggested age 9+ · medium space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** Tag kabaddi

**Equipment:** Cones, Tag belts

A raid is a tiny heist: cross the line, collect a tag, escape. The defenders must cooperate without crowding each other, while the raider decides how much risk is enough.

**Set up:** Mark two equal halves. Form two teams and give every player a removable tag belt. Choose a timekeeper.

1. Teams alternate sending one raider into the other half for up to 20 seconds.
2. The raider tries to remove defenders’ tags, then return across the centre line.
3. Defenders stop the raid by removing the raider’s tag. No tackling, holding or blocking with contact.
4. A safe return earns one point per tag collected. Losing the tag, stepping out or running out of time earns the defence one point instead.
5. Return all tags after each raid; rotate raiders so everyone has a turn.

**How it ends:** After equal numbers of raids, the higher total wins.

**The twist:** Every extra target makes the journey home more dangerous.

**Mix it up:** Intro adaptation: begin with one raider versus two defenders.

**Make it work:** Use a timer instead of breath holding; keep tags easy to remove and leave run-off space.

**House adaptation:** This designed tag-belt game removes tackling, breath control, eliminations and bonus-line rules from kabaddi.

### 39. Lagori

*Break the tower. Then rebuild it under pressure.*

India / South Asia · 6–16 people · 10–20 minutes · Suggested age 8+ · large space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** Seven stones, Pitthu, Satoliya, Ezhu Kallu

**Equipment:** Soft foam ball, 7 stackable foam discs, Cones

The same tower is first your target and then your responsibility. Once it falls, the neat throwing contest becomes a frantic rebuilding mission, with decoys buying precious seconds for the builders.

**Set up:** Stack seven discs inside a centre circle. Mark a throwing line and boundaries. One team attacks; the other fields.

1. Attackers take up to three throws total to topple the stack. If all miss, swap roles.
2. Once it falls, attackers scatter and try to rebuild all seven discs.
3. Defenders recover and pass the ball, trying to hit attackers gently below the knees. Hit players leave this round.
4. A complete stack plus the call “Lagori!” earns attackers one point. Putting all attackers out earns defenders one.
5. Reset the tower and swap roles after either result. Play six rounds.

**How it ends:** Most points after equal attacking turns wins.

**The twist:** The best throw creates the problem you now have to solve.

**Mix it up:** Intro adaptation: use five discs and roll the ball to tag feet.

**Make it work:** Use soft discs instead of stone or tile; no close-range throws at a builder.

**Starter rules:** This fixes three team attempts and alternating rounds; catches, attempts and finishes vary locally.

**Sources:**

- [Traditional Games Federation of India — Satoliya](<https://www.traditionalgamesindia.com/games-list/satoliya-the-seven-stone/>)

### 40. Peteca

*Badminton’s flight. Volleyball’s hands. Just one touch.*

Brazil · 2–4 people · 10–20 minutes · Suggested age 8+ · medium space · Energy 2/3

**Also known as:** —

**Equipment:** Peteca shuttle, Net, Court markers

A feathered shuttle slows dramatically after a strike. That gives you time to read its flight, but only one touch to send it back: doubles becomes a conversation about who will commit.

**Set up:** Mark a rectangular court divided by a net. Play singles or doubles, with the net at a comfortable height.

1. Serve by striking the peteca with one open hand from behind the baseline.
2. The receiving side gets one hand strike to return it over the net; no catching or teammate passes.
3. Landing in the opposite court earns a point. Out, a double touch or a failed return gives the other side a point.
4. For this short introduction, the rally winner serves next. Play to 11, winning by two; swap ends halfway.

**How it ends:** First side to win two short sets wins.

**The twist:** One touch makes every return a decision, not a setup pass.

**Mix it up:** Cooperative adaptation: remove the net and count consecutive group hand strikes.

**Make it work:** Use a light beginner peteca and avoid hard downward hits near faces.

**House adaptation:** One-touch mechanics are documented; rally scoring to 11 and winner-serves are our introductory format.

**Sources:**

- [Peteca International — Condensed rules](<https://peteca.net/rules/>)

### 41. Sepak takraw

*Three touches. No hands. A whole new net game.*

Southeast Asia · 6 people · 15–30 minutes · Suggested age 12+ · medium space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** Kick volleyball

**Equipment:** Soft takraw training ball, Net, Court markers

A foot becomes a setter’s hand and a knee becomes a receiving platform. The famous acrobatics are optional: a gentle three-touch rally already feels like discovering a new way to move.

**Set up:** Create a net court for two teams of three. Use a low net and soft training ball for a first session.

1. A teammate tosses the ball to the server, who kicks it over from a marked service spot.
2. Return it within three touches using feet, legs, torso or head; hands and arms cannot contact it.
3. One player may take more than one touch. Keep the ball airborne and stay on your side.
4. A grounded ball, out-ball, net contact or fourth touch gives opponents one point. Alternate the serving team after each point.
5. Play to 15; at 14–14, play to 17. Take a break and change ends between sets.

**How it ends:** Win two sets to win the match.

**The twist:** The three-touch pattern feels familiar; the body language is completely different.

**Mix it up:** Learning adaptation: allow one bounce per side, clearly agreed before starting.

**Make it work:** Start with low kicks and knee control; leave overhead kicks to coached practice.

**Starter rules:** Regu scoring follows the cited 2024 rules; training ball, net height and dimensions are introductory adjustments.

**Sources:**

- [Sepak Takraw Association of Canada — Regu rules](<https://www.sepaktakraw.ca/regu-sepak-takraw>)

### 42. Kī-o-rahi

*Two teams. Two entirely different ways to score.*

Aotearoa New Zealand / Māori · 10–14 people · 15–25 minutes · Suggested age 10+ · large space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** Ki o Rahi

**Equipment:** Soft ball, 7 outer markers, Central barrel, Cones, Tag belts

One team collects points around the outside, then tries to bank them in the middle. The other fires at a central target. The same circular field becomes two overlapping tactical worlds.

**Set up:** Mark an outer zone, inner ring and central target zone with a barrel (tupu). Put seven pou markers outside. Two Kīoma guards stay centrally; other Kīoma stay outside. Two Taniwha play the inner ring; others stay outside.

1. Kīoma run or pass outside, earning one pending point for each different pou touched with the ball; repeat touches add nothing.
2. A Kīoma carrier banks that total by carrying across the inner ring and grounding centrally. Only a scoring carrier may leave the outside, apart from the guards.
3. Taniwha pass into the inner ring and shoot at the tupu for one point. Kīoma guards may block or catch, without touching the barrel.
4. A removed carrier tag, drop, interception or out-ball hands possession to opponents at the outer edge and erases pending pou points. No passing during a Kīoma crossing.
5. After either side scores, opponents restart outside. Swap roles every five minutes, giving equal time in each role.

**How it ends:** After equal time in each role, the higher total wins.

**The twist:** There is no single shared goal: each side reads the field differently.

**Mix it up:** Learning adaptation: practise each scoring role separately before combining them.

**Make it work:** Use tag belts and a soft ball; mark a no-standing buffer around the barrel.

**House adaptation:** Designed teaching version: simplified zones, single-tag turnovers and alternating scoring restarts. Local ture differ; learn with Māori practitioners where possible.

**Sources:**

### 43. Lippa

*Flick it up. Hit it away. Measure in bat-lengths.*

Italy / European tip-cat traditions · 2–8 people · 15–25 minutes · Suggested age 12+ · large space · Energy 2/3

**Also known as:** S-cianco, Tip-cat

**Equipment:** Purpose-made lippa set, Measuring tape, Boundary markers

The ball is a short tapered stick, and the serve is a trick: strike its end so it jumps, then hit it in mid-air. Even one clean connection feels like a small invention.

**Set up:** Use a large, clear field and a marked hitting base. Everyone except the hitter waits behind a safety line.

1. Place the short lippa on its base with a tapered end accessible.
2. Tap that end with the longer bat to lift it, then strike it away in the air.
3. A complete miss uses an attempt. After a valid hit, wait until it stops and measure from the base.
4. Score one point per whole bat-length. Retrieve only on the leader’s signal.
5. Give every player five attempts and add their distances.

**How it ends:** Highest combined distance wins; tied players share the win.

**The twist:** You create your own airborne ball before you can hit it.

**Mix it up:** Documented variant: fielders can cancel a hit by catching; reserve this for suitable coached equipment and space.

**Make it work:** Flying wood needs a very large exclusion area; use purpose-made soft training equipment for beginners.

**Starter rules:** This uses FIGeST’s distance-only format, with five attempts; regional fielding and scoring versions also exist.

**Sources:**

- [FIGeST — Lippa technical sheet and rules](<https://figest.it/lippa/>)

### 44. Bete-ombro

*Two bats, two targets, and a race between them.*

Brazil · 4 people · 15–25 minutes · Suggested age 10+ · large space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** Taco, Bets, Bete

**Equipment:** 2 light bats, Soft ball, 2 plastic targets, Base markers

Two friends defend little targets at opposite ends. A successful hit gives both of them a reason to sprint. The fielders want more than an out: they want possession of the bats.

**Set up:** Set a target inside a small base circle at each end. Two batters defend one each; two fielders bowl from behind opposite ends.

1. A fielder bowls the soft ball towards the far target. Its batter tries to strike it away.
2. After a hit, batters may exchange ends while fielders retrieve. In this adaptation, each completed exchange scores one.
3. A grounded bat inside a base makes that batter safe. Fielders can knock over an unprotected target with the ball.
4. A toppled target or clean airborne catch swaps batting and fielding roles.
5. Continue from the new roles; announce and agree pauses before anyone leaves a base.

**How it ends:** First pair to ten runs wins after completing the last exchange.

**The twist:** The prize for good fielding is the right to become the batting team.

**Mix it up:** Intro adaptation: bowl underarm along the ground for easier first hits.

**Make it work:** Leave room for bat swings. No throws at runners, and no clashing bats while running.

**House adaptation:** This removes body-hit outs and bat-tapping during runs; catch rules and scoring vary around Brazil.

### 45. Ampe

*Clap. Jump. Choose a foot. Read your opponent.*

Ghana / West Africa · 2–20 people · 5–10 minutes · Suggested age 6+ · small space · Energy 2/3

**Also known as:** —

**Equipment:** None

This tiny rhythmic duel has no equipment and almost no waiting. You jump together, reveal a foot together, and discover whether you predicted each other or fell into the same pattern again.

**Set up:** Face a partner with room between you. One player scores for matching feet, the other for different feet; agree which is which.

1. Clap together to establish a shared beat.
2. On the beat, make a small jump and land with one foot forward.
3. If both chose left or both chose right, the matching player gets one point. Otherwise the different-feet player gets one.
4. Reset and repeat until someone reaches ten. Switch scoring roles for the next game.
5. For a group, rotate partners after every game and keep the rhythm going.

**How it ends:** First to ten wins a game; play both scoring roles.

**The twist:** Rock-paper-scissors becomes a whole-body rhythm.

**Mix it up:** Access adaptation: stay seated and reveal a left or right hand on the beat.

**Make it work:** Use soft knees and small jumps; feet never need to touch the other person.

**Starter rules:** This fixes matching/different scoring roles; names and tournament formats vary.

### 46. Pilolo

*A treasure hunt with a sprint finish.*

Ghana · 4–20 people · 5–15 minutes · Suggested age 6+ · medium space · Energy 2/3

**Also known as:** —

**Equipment:** Large coloured tokens, Start-line markers

The search is only half the game. Once you find an object, you still have to get home. A hiding place close to the finish can be more valuable than an obvious one.

**Set up:** Choose a leader and a bounded search area. The leader hides one large token per player while everyone waits out of sight.

1. Gather players behind a clear start-and-finish line.
2. On “Pilolo!”, search the agreed area for one token.
3. After finding one, return across the finish line carrying it. Do not take tokens from other players.
4. The first returning player wins that round; let everyone finish their search.
5. Change the leader, hide the tokens again and start another round.

**How it ends:** Play five rounds and count first finishes, or simply celebrate each round’s finder.

**The twist:** The best search route is also an escape route.

**Mix it up:** Cooperative adaptation: time how quickly the whole group brings every token home.

**Make it work:** Use visible boundaries and reachable hiding spots; no roads, climbing or tiny swallowable objects.

**Starter rules:** The search-and-return mechanic is documented; our five-round tally and large tokens are hosting choices.

### 47. Jianzi

*A feather, a foot, and one more impossible save.*

China / East Asia · 3–8 people · 5–15 minutes · Suggested age 7+ · small space · Energy 2/3

**Also known as:** Ti jianzi, 毽子

**Equipment:** Jianzi shuttlecock

The weighted feather drops differently from a ball: quick enough to demand attention, slow enough for a clever rescue. A circle becomes a shared little laboratory of ankles, knees and timing.

**Set up:** Stand in a loose circle on level ground with a jianzi. Leave room for each player to lift a leg comfortably.

1. Drop the shuttle towards a foot and kick it upward to begin.
2. Keep it airborne with feet, knees or other body parts except hands and arms.
3. Pass to another player whenever possible; count every successful contact aloud.
4. When it touches the ground, end the rally and begin again from zero with a different starter.

**How it ends:** Try to beat the group’s best rally within five minutes.

**The twist:** The score belongs to the whole circle, including whoever makes the save.

**Mix it up:** Learning adaptation: allow one catch to reset control, then restart counting after the next kick.

**Make it work:** Keep kicks low and upward, away from others’ faces; enlarge the circle as confidence grows.

**Starter rules:** This is cooperative circle kicking; competitive net shuttlecock has a separate rulebook.

### 48. Dibeke

*Kick once. Make the fielders chase a whole team.*

South Africa · 12–24 people · 15–25 minutes · Suggested age 10+ · large space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** Diwiki, Skaloulo

**Equipment:** Soft foam ball, Cones, Score sheet

A single kicker can send several teammates running between safe boxes. Fielders suddenly have many possible targets, while runners decide whether to stay safe or help create a distracting wave of movement.

**Set up:** Mark two safe boxes at opposite ends, a halfway line and a kicking area. Split into runners and fielders; choose a roller.

1. The roller calls a runner’s name and rolls the ball to the kicking area.
2. That player kicks beyond halfway and must run to the opposite safe box. Teammates may run too. Retry a miss or short kick; three failed kicks dismiss the kicker.
3. Fielders pass the foam ball and gently throw below runners’ knees. A hit outside a safe box puts that runner out for the innings.
4. Each completed crossing scores one run in this adaptation. Stop when everyone is safe or out; roll to the next runner.
5. Swap roles after everyone is out or five minutes. Give each team equal innings.

**How it ends:** Most total crossings after equal innings wins.

**The twist:** Teammates can flood the field to make one runner harder to stop.

**Mix it up:** Gentler adaptation: use hand tags by a stationary ball-holder instead of thrown tags.

**Make it work:** Use a soft ball only, with no close-range shots; safe boxes need room for the whole team.

**House adaptation:** Based on Dibeke’s kick-and-run structure; short equal innings and direct run totals replace the full scoring system.

**Sources:**

### 49. Tchoukball

*Two teams. Two targets. Both belong to everyone.*

Switzerland / international clubs · 8–14 people · 10–20 minutes · Suggested age 10+ · large space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** —

**Equipment:** Two rebound frames, Soft handball, Court markers

Your opponents must let you pass and shoot. Their job begins when the ball ricochets off the net. Suddenly, defence means reading geometry, and either end of the court can become your target.

**Set up:** Place a rebound frame at each end of a rectangular court, each inside a marked 3 m semicircle. Make two teams; begin outside the semicircles.

1. Pass freely without interference. Hold for at most three seconds, take at most three steps, and shoot within three team passes.
2. Shoot at either frame. After three consecutive shots at one frame, the next shot must use the other.
3. Score one if the rebound lands in court outside the semicircle. A defender catching it starts their team's attack.
4. A shot missing the frame, rebounding out, or landing inside the semicircle gives the opposition one point. Dropped passes give them possession.
5. After a point, the conceding team restarts behind that baseline. After a passing fault, touch the ball to the floor before passing.

**How it ends:** Play a 10-minute introduction; most points wins.

**The twist:** Nobody owns a goal, and intercepting is forbidden.

**Mix it up:** Teaching adaptation: begin with one frame and alternate attacks.

**Make it work:** Secure frames; leave landing room. Start with standing shots and no dives.

**Starter rules:** Condensed introduction; consult the federation rules for frame faults and competition details.

**Sources:**

- [Tchoukball UK — How to Play](<https://www.tchoukball.org.uk/how-to-play/>)

### 50. KIN-BALL

*Call a colour. Launch a very big problem.*

Québec, Canada / international clubs · 12 people · 10–20 minutes · Suggested age 10+ · large space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** Kinball, Omnikin

**Equipment:** KIN-BALL sport ball, Three colours of bibs, Court markers

Three teams share one court and an enormous lightweight ball. You only become the receiving team when your colour is called, so anticipation matters just as much as the frantic collective catch.

**Set up:** Make three teams of four, each with a colour. Clear a generous indoor court; keep everyone away from walls and overhead fittings.

1. Three teammates support the ball; the fourth calls 'Omnikin' and another team's colour before hitting it.
2. Send the ball outward or upward, never downward. For this introduction, require it to travel at least 3 m.
3. The called team must control it before it reaches the floor. Everyone else gives them space.
4. After a successful catch, that team prepares the next hit. Rotate hitters so one player never hits twice consecutively.
5. If a team drops the ball, sends it out, or commits a fault, both other teams score one. The faulting team restarts.

**How it ends:** After 10 minutes, the team with the most points wins; a tie may stand.

**The twist:** Every error helps two opponents at once.

**Mix it up:** Teaching adaptation: call the colour before setting the ball to give receivers extra time.

**Make it work:** Use the proper lightweight ball. Practise group catches; no body contact or blocking.

**Starter rules:** Teaching format with a 3 m minimum; competitive call restrictions, timing and period scoring are more detailed.

<a id="ultimate"></a>

### 51. Ultimate

*The disc flies. The thrower stays put.*

United States / international clubs · 6–14 people · 10–25 minutes · Suggested age 9+ · large space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** Ultimate Frisbee, Flying disc

**Equipment:** Flying disc, Eight cones

A beautifully floating pass can do what sprinting cannot: carry the attack across the field. Everyone can become the next receiver, and players share responsibility for making honest calls.

**Set up:** Mark a rectangular field with an end zone at each end. Split into equal teams; scale the field to the group's throwing range.

1. Start on opposite end-zone lines. The defending team throws the disc to the attackers to begin the point.
2. Catch, stop, and pivot to pass. You cannot run with the disc; teammates move to find space.
3. A nearby marker counts ten seconds aloud. A drop, interception, out-of-bounds pass or completed stall turns possession over immediately.
4. Catch a teammate's pass inside the opponents' end zone for one point. Change ends; the scoring team throws the next opening disc.
5. Avoid contact and resolve disputed calls together. If a foul call is contested, replay the affected pass.

**How it ends:** For a short game, first to five points wins.

**The twist:** Movement belongs to the receivers; fair play belongs to everyone.

**Mix it up:** Teaching adaptation: allow 15 seconds to throw while beginners learn to pivot.

**Make it work:** Use a soft disc with beginners; leave clear run-off space and avoid diving near others.

**Starter rules:** Small-sided introduction using USA Ultimate's basic rules, with a shorter scoring target.

**Sources:**

- [USA Ultimate — Ultimate in 10 Simple Rules](<https://usaultimate.org/rules/10-simple-rules/>)

<a id="jugger"></a>

### 52. Jugger

*Escort your runner through a forest of foam.*

Germany / international Jugger communities · 10 people · 10–20 minutes · Suggested age 14+ · large space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** Foam Jugger, Jugg

**Equipment:** Checked padded Jugger spars, Foam ball, Two ground goals, Beat timer

Only one player per team can score. Everyone else clears a route with carefully controlled foam tags, while a steady beat decides when tagged players can return to the action.

**Set up:** Two teams each choose one unarmed runner and four spar players. Put a foam ball at centre and a ground goal near each baseline.

1. On 'Jugger!', runners try to collect the ball. Only runners may carry it or place it in the opposing goal.
2. Spar players use gentle padded tags on agreed body zones below the shoulders. This introduction excludes chains, wrestling and pinning.
3. A tagged player raises a hand, kneels if comfortable, and counts five audible beats, 1.5 seconds apart. Then they rejoin.
4. A tagged runner immediately puts the ball down. Simultaneous tags deactivate both players; nobody grabs or blocks bodies.
5. Placing the ball in the opposing goal scores one. Reset everyone to baselines and the ball to centre. Rotate runners between points.

**How it ends:** First to three points wins the introductory match.

**The twist:** The scorer has no spar; the protectors cannot score.

**Mix it up:** Teaching adaptation: try walking Jugger before increasing the pace.

**Make it work:** Learn with an experienced facilitator and inspected equipment. Exclude head, neck, hands and groin; stop on hard contact.

**House adaptation:** Recreational introduction derived from Jugger; several contact and equipment rules are deliberately removed.

**Sources:**

- [Swedish Jugger Association — Rules of the Game](<https://jugger.se/en/about-jugger/rules-of-the-game/>)

<a id="roundnet"></a>

### 53. Roundnet

*Volleyball turned sideways, with no side to defend.*

International clubs / parks and beaches · 4 people · 10–20 minutes · Suggested age 10+ · medium space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** Spikeball

**Equipment:** Roundnet set, Roundnet ball

The net lies flat like a tiny trampoline. Once the serve bounces, the whole circle becomes available, and an innocent set can turn into a shot behind everyone's backs.

**Set up:** Put the round net on level ground with clear space all around. Two pairs stand around it; the server faces the receiver.

1. Serve by striking the ball down onto the net so it rebounds towards the receiver. Use easy serves to begin.
2. The receiving pair has up to three alternating touches to send the ball back onto the net. No catching or carrying.
3. After the serve, move around all 360 degrees. Give opponents room to reach the ball; replay an obstructed rally.
4. A ground touch, rim hit, double bounce on the net, or failure to return within three touches gives the opponents one point.
5. The point-winning team serves next; alternate your team's server when regaining service. Reset positions for each serve.

**How it ends:** Play to 11, winning by two, for a short introductory game.

**The twist:** There is no permanent 'your side' once a rally starts.

**Mix it up:** Teaching adaptation: permit one catch-and-toss as the first touch.

**Make it work:** Do not jump into the net or dive into another player's path.

**House adaptation:** Casual starter format; current competition rules add service, boundary and no-drop-zone details.

**Sources:**

- [Australian Roundnet Association — Roundnet Basics](<https://roundnet.com.au/pages/go-play>)

<a id="danish-longball"></a>

### 54. Danish Longball

*One faraway safe base. A very tempting journey home.*

Scandinavia / school games · 8–24 people · 15–25 minutes · Suggested age 9+ · large space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** Swedish longball, Longball

**Equipment:** Foam bat, Soft ball, Cones, Two base areas

The safe base can fill with a whole crowd of runners, all waiting for the same opening. The fielders must decide whether to pursue an out or shut the journey down.

**Set up:** Mark a home base and a runner base roughly 10–20 m apart. Split into batting and fielding teams; assign a pitcher and a batting order.

1. The pitcher tosses underarm. After one swing, hit or miss, place the bat down and run to the far base.
2. A runner may wait at the far base with others, then attempt a return when play is live. Once returning, keep going.
3. An airborne catch puts the batter out; a gentle ball-in-hand tag puts a runner out between bases. Never throw at runners.
4. Fielders can return the ball to the pitcher, who calls 'Freeze'. In this version, untagged runners return to their last safe base.
5. Each completed out-and-back run scores one. Three outs switch the teams; runners still waiting are cleared.

**How it ends:** Play three equal batting innings each; highest total wins.

**The twist:** Several runners can share the safe base and burst home together.

**Mix it up:** Use a batting tee so everyone can make a controlled first hit.

**Make it work:** Mark a bat exclusion area. Fielders and waiting batters stay outside it.

**House adaptation:** Based on a PE teaching version; the freeze/reset rule is explicitly simplified here.

<a id="newcomb"></a>

### 55. Newcomb

*Catch your breath. Then find the empty space.*

School and recreation courts worldwide · 4–12 people · 10–20 minutes · Suggested age 6+ · medium space · Energy 2/3

**Also known as:** Newcombe ball, Catch volleyball

**Equipment:** Soft ball, Low net or visible ribbon, Court markers

The satisfying tactics of volleyball arrive before the tricky ball skills. Catching gives everyone a moment to scan the other court, spot the gap, and choose where the next problem lands.

**Set up:** Split a small rectangular court with a low net. Make equal teams, spreading players across each half.

1. A player behind the back line throws the ball over the net to start. The other side catches it before it lands.
2. The catcher stays in place and throws back within three seconds. Keep feet on the ground; no blocking or spiking.
3. For this simple version, the catcher returns directly rather than passing to teammates.
4. If a ball lands inside the opposing court, score one. A throw into the net, out of bounds, or dropped catch gives the other team one.
5. The team that won the point serves. Rotate the server each time your team gains service so everyone has a turn.

**How it ends:** First to 11 points wins; no player is eliminated.

**The twist:** Catching changes the pace, but placement still wins rallies.

**Mix it up:** Progress to a caught first contact followed by a volleyball-style return.

**Make it work:** Use a light ball and lower the net to suit throwing reach. Keep the net clearly visible.

**Starter rules:** A simple catch-and-throw teaching version; Newcomb scoring and passing conventions vary.

**Sources:**

- [Coaching Volleyball — Game: Newcomb](<https://coachingvb.com/game-newcomb/>)

<a id="endball"></a>

### 56. Endball

*Your goal is a teammate who can catch.*

School PE / invasion-game teaching · 6–12 people · 8–15 minutes · Suggested age 7+ · medium space · Energy 2/3

**Also known as:** End ball, Goalie endball

**Equipment:** Soft ball, Court markers, Team bibs

Instead of aiming at a fixed goal, find your teammate sliding along the end line. Everyone must contribute to the attack, so the quiet player in space can be the crucial link.

**Set up:** Mark a rectangle with an end line at each end. Each team stations one catcher behind the opposite end line; others play inside.

1. Start with one team at its own end. Pass underarm through the court; the ball-holder stays still in this version.
2. Everyone on the playing court must touch the ball during the possession before your team can score.
3. Defenders can intercept passes but cannot touch the ball-holder or knock the ball from their hands.
4. A legal pass caught by your end-line catcher scores one. The catcher may move along the line while staying behind it.
5. Rotate the scoring team's catcher, then give the other team a restart. Dropped or out-of-bounds passes also turn possession over.

**How it ends:** Play eight minutes; highest score wins.

**The twist:** The target moves, and every teammate has to join the passing chain.

**Mix it up:** Teaching adaptation: remove the all-touch condition for a first practice round.

**Make it work:** Give the catcher clear space behind the line. Match passes to the receiver's reach.

**Starter rules:** Based on the University of Victoria activity; possession restarts and stationary passing are specified for this introduction.

**Sources:**

- [University of Victoria — Endball Activity Sheet](<https://web.uvic.ca/~thopper/tactic/Units/KristinaJamie/PE452%20Project/activity%20sheets/endball.html>)

<a id="speedball"></a>

### 57. Speedball

*The ball hits the floor. The sport changes.*

United States / school PE · 8–16 people · 10–20 minutes · Suggested age 10+ · large space · Energy 3/3

**Also known as:** Soccer-handball speedball

**Equipment:** Soft football-sized ball, Two cone goals, End-zone markers

One moment you are playing a passing game with your hands. A dropped ball instantly turns it into football, until a carefully lofted kick brings it back into someone's hands again.

**Set up:** Mark a rectangle with a cone goal and a shallow end zone at each end. Make two equal teams; begin with a free pass at centre.

1. A ball on the ground must be played with feet. Dribble or pass; nobody simply picks it up.
2. A controlled kick caught before touching the ground turns it into an aerial ball. Now teammates may throw and catch.
3. A ball-holder must pass, shoot or drop it within three seconds or three steps. A drop immediately restores feet-only play.
4. In this version, kick a ground ball through the opposing cone goal for three points, or catch a pass in their end zone for one.
5. After scoring, the other team takes a free pass at centre. Interceptions change possession; out-of-bounds balls give opponents a free pass at that edge.

**How it ends:** After 10 minutes, the higher score wins.

**The twist:** The ball's relationship with the ground selects the rules.

**Mix it up:** Teaching adaptation: make every score worth one while learning the transitions.

**Make it work:** No high kicks, slide tackles or body contact. Loft passes only into clear space.

**House adaptation:** Compact version of school Speedball; basketball scoring and advanced kicks are omitted.

**Sources:**

- [Cranford High School — Speedball Study Guide](<https://chs.cranfordschools.org/athletics/pe_links/speedball.html>)

<a id="korfball"></a>

### 58. Korfball

*Before you can shoot, you have to get free.*

Netherlands / international clubs · 16 people · 10–20 minutes · Suggested age 10+ · large space · Energy 2/3

**Also known as:** Korfbal

**Equipment:** Two korfball posts, Ball, Court markers, Team bibs

A basket without a backboard can be attacked from every direction. But a well-positioned defender makes a shot illegal, so clever passing and movement matter more than simply shooting over someone.

**Set up:** Use two court halves with a basket in each. Each eight-player team puts four attackers in one half and four defenders in the other.

1. Stay in your assigned half. Move the ball by passing; do not run or dribble while holding it.
2. In this introduction, agree matched defending pairs before play. Defenders may intercept but cannot take a held ball or make contact.
3. A shot is defended if the assigned opponent faces you, stands between you and the basket within arm's reach, and actively tries to block it.
4. An undefended shot through the basket scores one. A defended shot gives possession to the defence, even if it goes in.
5. The conceding team restarts at centre after a score. After every two total goals, swap halves and attacking/defending roles.

**How it ends:** Play 10 minutes; most goals wins.

**The twist:** Good defence removes the shot itself, not just its chance of success.

**Mix it up:** Use lower baskets to make passing and finding space the main challenge.

**Make it work:** Secure posts and leave clearance around them. Agree balanced matchups collaboratively.

**House adaptation:** Introductory matched-pair adaptation; official Korfball uses mixed teams and same-gender defending rules.

### 59. Sitting Volleyball

*Lower the net. Discover a completely different rally.*

International ParaVolley clubs · 6–12 people · 10–20 minutes · Suggested age 9+ · medium space · Energy 2/3

**Also known as:** Sitzvolleyball, Volley assis

**Equipment:** Volleyball or light training ball, Low net, Court markers

The floor becomes part of the technique. Quick hand-supported movement, low angles and a net close to eye level make familiar volleyball decisions feel wonderfully different, even to experienced standing players.

**Set up:** Use a smooth, clean court up to 10 × 6 m with a low net around 1.1 m. Start seated, with equal teams on each side.

1. Serve from behind your end line. Keep part of your torso in contact with the floor when playing the ball in this introduction.
2. Use up to three team contacts to return it over the net. Do not catch or throw; consecutive contacts by one player are normally faults.
3. Players move along the floor to cover space. Blocking the serve is allowed, unlike in standing volleyball.
4. A ball landing inside the opposition's court scores one. A fault or ball out gives the opponents one; every rally scores.
5. The point winner serves. When service changes teams, rotate the serving order so everyone participates.

**How it ends:** For an introduction, first to 15 points with a two-point lead wins.

**The twist:** A lower viewpoint changes both the attack angles and the movement skills.

**Mix it up:** Teaching adaptation: permit a catch on the first contact with a soft training ball.

**Make it work:** Check floor comfort and shoulder load. Agree suitable movement adaptations; do not assume sitting suits every body.

**Starter rules:** Short recreational set; full ParaVolley rules contain contact exceptions and competition eligibility details.

<a id="hooverball"></a>

### 60. Hooverball

*Catch, return, repeat. No teammate can bail you out.*

United States / historic White House exercise game · 4–8 people · 10–20 minutes · Suggested age 12+ · medium space · Energy 2/3

**Also known as:** Hoover-Ball, Light-ball Hooverball

**Equipment:** Light foam training ball, Net, Court markers

Originally a medicine-ball workout associated with President Herbert Hoover, this catch-and-return game offers a surprising placement puzzle: a catch near the net must be returned deep. Here, start with a light ball.

**Set up:** Divide a net court into front and back halves on each side. Make teams of two to four; use a comfortable net height.

1. Serve from behind the back line by throwing the ball over the net.
2. Catch a return before it lands and throw it straight back from where you caught it. No running or teammate passes.
3. If you caught it in your front half, your return must reach the opponents' back half. A back-half catch may target either half.
4. A dropped catch, throw into the net or out, or short front-half return gives the other team a point.
5. For this light-ball version, the team winning the rally serves next; rotate its server after each point.

**How it ends:** Play to 11 points with a two-point lead.

**The twist:** Your catching position determines where you are allowed to throw.

**Mix it up:** Try the historical tennis scoring once everyone knows the placement rule.

**Make it work:** This version uses a light ball. Historical medicine-ball play demands different strength and catching preparation.

**House adaptation:** Light-ball adaptation with rally scoring; historical rules use a medicine ball and tennis scoring.

**Sources:**

- [U.S. National Park Service — Hoover-Ball](<https://www.nps.gov/teachers/classrooms/hooverball.htm>)

### 61. Hoop Pass

*A whole group, threaded through one hoop.*

Playgrounds / cooperative group games · 6–16 people · 5–10 minutes · Suggested age 6+ · small space · Energy 1/3

**Also known as:** Hula Hoop Challenge, Hula Hoop Pass

**Equipment:** Large hula hoop

The hoop looks impossibly small once everyone's hands are linked. The solution is a shared little choreography: step, duck, lift, pause, and make the next person's turn easier than your own.

**Set up:** Form a comfortably spaced circle. With everyone's agreement, link hands and place a hoop over one joined pair before closing the circle.

1. Move the hoop to the next person without breaking the chain of hands.
2. Each person carefully passes their body through the hoop, with neighbours helping by lifting or lowering their joined hands.
3. Keep going in the same direction until the hoop returns to its starting link.
4. Pause if anyone needs to release their grip. Reset comfortably and continue; nobody is eliminated.
5. Discuss the smoothest movement, then try a second circuit together.

**How it ends:** The whole group succeeds when the hoop completes a circuit.

**The twist:** Helping your neighbour is part of moving your own body through.

**Mix it up:** Add a second hoop travelling in the opposite direction once one circuit feels easy.

**Make it work:** Never pull arms or force someone through. Use an opening hoop or pass it around a seated person's reach as agreed.

**Documented game:** A cooperative challenge; stopping or releasing for comfort is always allowed.

### 62. Ninja

*One move. One dodge. Freeze in your ridiculous pose.*

Camp and youth-group circles · 4–12 people · 5–10 minutes · Suggested age 8+ · medium space · Energy 2/3

**Also known as:** Ninja Slap, Hand-tap Ninja

**Equipment:** None

Part reaction test, part living sculpture. Every attack leaves the group frozen in a new arrangement, and you must wait for your next turn while planning around everyone else's improbable poses.

**Set up:** Form a loose circle with plenty of space. Agree the turn order and use only gentle taps to the back of a hand.

1. Count down together, then each take one step into a starting pose and freeze.
2. On your turn, make one continuous movement to tap another player's hand, or reposition yourself.
3. Only the targeted player may make one dodging movement. Both players then freeze in their new poses.
4. A player whose hand is tapped leaves this short round. Continue with the next active player in the original turn order.
5. When one player remains, everyone rejoins and the starting player moves one place around the circle.

**How it ends:** The last player remaining wins the round.

**The twist:** Your awkward finishing pose is the next person's tactical opportunity.

**Mix it up:** House version: each hand is one life; retire only the tapped hand until both are out.

**Make it work:** Tap rather than slap. No grabs, head contact, kicks or wild jumps; seated play works with a shared reach boundary.

**Starter rules:** Hand-only, one-hit-out version; many groups use two hands or different target zones.

### 63. Zip Zap Zop

*An invisible ball made entirely of attention.*

Theatre warm-ups / group workshops · 6–20 people · 3–8 minutes · Suggested age 6+ · small space · Energy 1/3

**Also known as:** Zip Zap, Theatre energy circle

**Equipment:** None

A sound and a gesture travel from person to person as though the circle shares one nervous system. The fun is finding a collective rhythm, then watching it wobble when someone gets surprised.

**Set up:** Stand or sit in a circle where everyone can see one another. Practise a clear, comfortable passing gesture.

1. One player gestures clearly towards another and says 'Zip'. Make sure the receiver knows it is for them.
2. That receiver sends 'Zap' to someone else; the next sends 'Zop'.
3. Continue the repeating Zip–Zap–Zop sequence, sending it across the circle in unpredictable directions.
4. If the order breaks or nobody knows who received it, laugh, pause and restart at 'Zip' with the last receiver.
5. Try to build 30 uninterrupted passes together before ending the round.

**How it ends:** The group succeeds at its shared uninterrupted-pass target; nobody goes out.

**The twist:** The rhythm is fixed, but the recipient is always a surprise.

**Mix it up:** Once confident, add a clap to every pass or replace the three sounds together.

**Make it work:** Name the receiver if visual cues are hard to follow. Agree a comfortable volume and pace.

**Starter rules:** Cooperative version with an added practice target; local Zip/Zap/Boing games may use different rules.

### 64. Roshambo Rockstar

*Lose a duel. Gain a whole new team.*

Playgrounds / large-group icebreakers · 8–60 people · 5–10 minutes · Suggested age 6+ · medium space · Energy 2/3

**Also known as:** Rock Paper Scissors Cheerleader, RPS Cheer Tournament

**Equipment:** None

A tiny hand game grows into an absurd sporting final. Every defeated player joins the winner's supporters, so the last two contestants arrive surrounded by whole crowds who want them to win.

**Set up:** Spread out in pairs. Agree to reveal on 'shoot' and review rock beats scissors, scissors beats paper, paper beats rock.

1. Partners introduce themselves and play one deciding round; replay ties.
2. The winner looks for another winner to challenge. Their partner becomes a supporter and follows at a comfortable distance.
3. After each duel, the losing player and their whole supporter group join the new winner's supporters.
4. Keep combining groups until only two competitors remain. Everyone supports one finalist.
5. Play the final duel, celebrate both players, then mix partners and restart from individual pairs.

**How it ends:** The last duel produces the round's rockstar; everyone remains part of the event.

**The twist:** The support teams grow precisely because people lose.

**Mix it up:** Use silent jazz hands and signed encouragement for a quieter tournament.

**Make it work:** No grabbing or crowding the finalists. Keep cheering friendly and allow participants to watch without chanting.

**Documented game:** The Playworks version is also called Rock Paper Scissors Cheerleader.

### 65. Parachute Popcorn

*Everyone owns one tiny piece of the weather.*

Scout groups / cooperative play · 6–24 people · 5–10 minutes · Suggested age 5+ · medium space · Energy 2/3

**Also known as:** Popcorn parachute

**Equipment:** Play parachute or strong sheet, Soft lightweight balls

One person can make a ripple, but a whole circle can make a storm. The balls bounce like popcorn while the group discovers how timing, direction and shared movement change what happens.

**Set up:** Spread around a parachute with comfortable spacing, holding its edge near waist height. Place several soft, lightweight balls in the centre.

1. Practise lifting and lowering the edge together. Agree a clear stop signal.
2. On 'Pop!', shake the parachute to bounce the balls. Keep hold of the edge and stay in your place.
3. Work together to pop every ball over the edge. Use the parachute rather than your hands to steer them.
4. When the final ball leaves, lower the parachute and stop. Only then collect the balls.
5. Refill and try another round with a different shared rhythm.

**How it ends:** The group succeeds when all the popcorn has left the parachute.

**The twist:** The only way to control the centre is by coordinating the edge.

**Mix it up:** Use two ball colours: each team tries to pop the other colour off while keeping its own aboard.

**Make it work:** Use soft balls and comfortable arm movements. A seated circle works; do not wrap fabric around hands.

**Documented game:** Popcorn's goal varies; this is the pop-everything-off version described by Scouts.

### 66. Human Knot

*A puzzle where the pieces have opinions.*

Camps / cooperative group challenges · 6–12 people · 5–15 minutes · Suggested age 8+ · small space · Energy 1/3

**Also known as:** Gordian knot, Group knot

**Equipment:** None

Everyone is connected, but nobody can see the whole solution from where they stand. A successful untangling feels less like winning a race and more like solving a little mystery together.

**Set up:** Form a small circle with an even number of willing participants. Agree that anybody can release a grip whenever they need to.

1. Each person joins right hands with someone who is not next to them, then left hands with a different person.
2. Look at the connections together. Propose one movement at a time: a turn, a step through or a carefully lifted pair of hands.
3. Try to untangle without changing partners. Move slowly and never pull or twist someone into position.
4. The facilitator may allow two 'cuts': briefly release one connection, make a move, then restore the same connection.
5. Finish when untangled, or agree why further movement is impossible. Release, discuss and build a fresh knot.

**How it ends:** Untangle into one or more loops, or explain together why this knot cannot be untied safely.

**The twist:** Your own route only works if your neighbours can move with it.

**Mix it up:** Hold short scarves between partners to give everyone extra space and avoid direct hand contact.

**Make it work:** Participation is optional. Release immediately for discomfort; no lifting people or forced ducking.

**Documented game:** Some knots do not resolve to a single circle; recognizing that is a valid outcome.

### 68. Balloon Bop

*One balloon. One circle. A shared record to beat.*

Cooperative classroom and group play · 4–12 people · 5–10 minutes · Suggested age 6+ · small space · Energy 1/3

**Also known as:** Group keepy-uppy, Balloon keep-up

A drifting balloon turns a simple circle into a moving team. Every gentle touch buys a little thinking time, and the group learns that frantic individual saves are not always the best solution.

3. Count each touch aloud. If the balloon lands or the chain breaks, pause and reset the count.
4. Plan how to cover a drifting balloon, then start another attempt.
5. After a few rounds, finish with one shared target, such as 20 touches.

**How it ends:** Beat the group's previous record or reach the agreed touch target.

**The twist:** The whole circle must respond to one floating object.

**Mix it up:** Unlinked adaptation: stay in assigned spots and call a teammate before sending the balloon towards them.

**Make it work:** Use a latex-free beach ball if needed. No chasing into furniture; stop and reset. Keep burst balloon pieces away from young children.

**Starter rules:** Based on Balloon Bop; this introduction omits head contact and allows an unlinked option.

### 69. Group Juggle

*Remember one pass. Survive five flying objects.*

Team-building workshops / camps · 6–20 people · 5–12 minutes · Suggested age 7+ · small space · Energy 1/3

**Also known as:** Name juggle, Pattern ball

**Equipment:** Three to five soft throwable objects

Each person has just one simple job: send the ball to the same teammate. Add another ball, then another, and that easy promise becomes a wonderfully busy network of names, timing and trust.

**Set up:** Stand or sit in a circle, with soft balls or beanbags ready. Begin with just one object.

1. Say somebody's name and make a gentle underarm toss to them. They choose someone who has not yet received it.
2. Continue until every person has caught once; the last receiver returns it to the starter.
3. Repeat exactly that passing order until everyone remembers who sends to them and whom they send to.
4. Start the sequence again and introduce a second object behind the first. Add more only when the group is ready.
5. A drop pauses that object's journey: retrieve it and resume from the same player. Finish when every object completes its circuit.

**How it ends:** Complete a circuit with the group's chosen number of objects in motion.

**The twist:** A fixed pattern becomes unpredictable when several objects share it.

**Mix it up:** Send one distinctively coloured object around the pattern in reverse.

**Make it work:** Call and check readiness before throwing. Use soft objects; roll them across a table as an alternative.

**Starter rules:** The reset and finish conditions make the documented open-ended activity into a short round.

**Sources:**

- [Texas Juvenile Justice Department — Group Juggle](<https://www.tjjd.texas.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/group-juggle.pdf>)

<a id="giants-wizards-elves"></a>

### 70. Giants, Wizards & Elves

*Rock-paper-scissors, performed by your entire team.*

Scout groups / party games · 6–40 people · 5–12 minutes · Suggested age 5+ · medium space · Energy 2/3

**Also known as:** Giants Elves Wizards, Team rock paper scissors

**Equipment:** None

The decision is tiny, but the reveal is magnificent. A whole team becomes growling giants or squeaking elves together, while trying to guess what the equally theatrical people opposite are planning.

**Set up:** Make two teams facing each other with a clear gap. Practise three actions: tall giant, spell-casting wizard and pointy-eared elf.

1. Learn the cycle: giants beat elves, elves beat wizards, and wizards beat giants.
2. Each team huddles and secretly agrees on one character. Everyone on that team must reveal the same choice.
3. Return to the facing lines. On a shared countdown, perform your chosen action and sound without crossing the gap.
4. The winning character earns its team one point. A tie earns neither team a point; huddle and choose again.
5. Repeat for seven rounds, changing who leads the huddle each time.

**How it ends:** Most points after seven rounds wins; tied teams share the win.

**The twist:** The bluff is collective, and the reveal uses your whole body.

**Mix it up:** Invent three new characters together, keeping the same circular win relationship.

**Make it work:** Offer small or seated gestures. Keep sounds at a comfortable level and avoid physical 'squashing'.

**Documented game:** This is the Scouts points version; chase-and-capture versions also circulate.

**Sources:**

- [Scouts — Play Giants, Wizards and Elves](<https://www.scouts.org.uk/activities/giants-wizards-and-elves>)

<a id="wink-murder"></a>

### 71. Wink Murder

*A detective, a circle, and one suspicious eyelid.*

Camp circles / parlour games · 7–20 people · 5–12 minutes · Suggested age 8+ · small space · Energy 1/3

**Also known as:** Wink detective, Wink assassin

**Equipment:** None

The smallest possible signal sets off the biggest theatrical reaction. The detective watches a circle of apparently innocent faces while the secret winker tries to act without giving themselves away.

**Set up:** Sit in a circle. Choose a detective to look away while a facilitator discreetly selects the winker.

1. The detective returns to the centre. Everyone looks around naturally; nobody announces who the winker is.
2. The winker makes eye contact and winks at one player at a time, trying to avoid the detective's attention.
3. A winked player pauses briefly, then performs a seated dramatic exit and folds their arms to show they are out of the round.
4. The detective has three guesses. A correct guess ends the round; three wrong guesses let the winker win.
5. For a short round, also stop after two minutes. Reveal the winker, rotate roles and invite everyone back in.

**How it ends:** The detective identifies the winker within three guesses and the time limit; otherwise the winker wins.

**The twist:** A delayed reaction can protect the person who just put you out.

**Mix it up:** Use a visible hand signal instead of a wink and call it Secret Signal.

**Make it work:** Keep exits seated rather than falling. Agree alternative cues if eye contact or winking is difficult.

**Starter rules:** Three-guess Scouts version with a short timer added to keep spectators involved.

**Sources:**

- [Scouts — Wink Murder](<https://www.scouts.org.uk/activities/wink-murder/>)

<a id="big-wind-blows"></a>

### 72. The Big Wind Blows

*One shared fact rearranges the whole room.*

Group workshops / camp icebreakers · 8–24 people · 5–10 minutes · Suggested age 7+ · medium space · Energy 2/3

**Also known as:** A Cold Wind Blows, Move If, Trainwreck

**Equipment:** Flat floor markers

An ordinary sentence becomes a sudden map of things you have in common. People change places, a new caller appears, and the circle slowly discovers its unexpected shared tastes and experiences.

**Set up:** Arrange a circle with one fewer marked spot than players. One player begins in the middle; keep the centre and paths clear.

1. The caller says, 'The big wind blows for everyone who…' and adds a light fact that is true about themselves.
2. Everyone who shares it walks to a different open spot, more than one position away. The caller also tries to claim a spot.
3. The player left without a spot becomes the new caller. Nobody is eliminated; if nobody else moves, the caller tries a new statement.
4. If two people reach one spot together, pause and agree who stays; the other becomes caller.
5. Continue until several people have called or five minutes have passed, then finish with one thing the group discovered.

**How it ends:** There is no overall winner; the aim is movement and discovering common ground.

**The twist:** A personal detail changes who moves, not who is 'out'.

**Mix it up:** Agree a 'Hurricane!' call that asks everyone to change spots.

**Make it work:** Walk, avoid personal disclosures, and allow a pass. Agree a stationary signal option for anyone who prefers to stay seated.

**Starter rules:** Floor-marker, walking version of the documented workshop game.

**Sources:**

- [Training for Change — The Big Wind Blows](<https://www.trainingforchange.org/training_tools/the-big-wind-blows/>)
